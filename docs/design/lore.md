Status: task LORE1, written 2026-09-28 for the owner's ask: "An agent building the lore and story of
the world. I'd like there to be a reason for us fighting these monsters." Docs only; no code.

This file is the single source of truth for the world's story. It builds on everything already
written in the game (Appendix A lists those facts) and contradicts none of it. Where old lines

## 0. The story in one breath

**The premise.** Ten years ago a voice in the far dark sang, and every lantern light in the land
rose out of its lamp and flew away to it. People called that night the Lanternfall, and the land
took the name. Since then the dark has crept out of caves, marsh and sea, and it changes what it
touches. You carry a small lamp that still holds. Its light catches where no other will, so you
walk the old Lantern Road and light the lamps again, one region at a time.

**Why we fight.** The monsters are the land's own moss, bats, stone, dead and drowned, soaked
through by the dark. The dark in them aches for light, so they come for the lamp you carry. Beat
one and the dark goes out of it: a slime is only moss again, and the dead lie still. Every fight
gives a little of the land back.

**The long mystery.** What waits at the end of the Lantern Road? The player learns it in layers
over weeks and months: who put the lights out (a Voice, not a storm), where the stolen light went
(the Emberwaste burns with it), why your lamp still holds (it was given to you), and at last who
the Voice is, and where. The answer is closer to home than anyone thinks (8.6).

**The heart of it, in one line:** *a light is safest when it is given away.* Light kept for no one
can be stolen. Light lit for someone cannot. That one rule explains the lamps that held, why your
lamp works, why Bonds matter, and how the story ends.

## 1. Tone and voice

**Warm, hopeful, a little melancholy.** People have lost things, and they talk about it quietly.
They are still kind, still funny, and they still light the fire every night. Nobody is cruel for
fun. Nothing is gory. The dark is sad and cold, not evil for its own sake.

House rules for every line (they match CLAUDE.md and the existing writing):

- Short sentences. Active voice. Plain words. Name things the way a player would.
- Show one small true thing, not a paragraph of history. ("He counts them sometimes, by the fire.")
- Leave one thing unsaid. Most existing stories end on a quiet turn; keep doing that.
- Speech is short, in double quotes, and sounds like a person, not a prophecy.
- Humour is dry and gentle (Pip, Tobin, Morwen's candles). Never mock the player.
- No grimdark: no torture, no gore, no children harmed on the page, no despair without a hand held
  out. Loss happened in the past; the present is about relighting.
- The dark is "the dark". The voice in it is "the Voice" (only once the player has earned the name;
  before that, "a voice", "something", "it"). Never invent a grand title for it in player text.
- No capitalised jargon the player has not met. A new term arrives with its meaning in the same line.

Length limits (the delivery channels in section 9 use these):

| Kind | Limit |
|---|---|
| Bark, Voice line, Hand talk, Omen line | under 60 characters |
| Arrival line, bestiary line, bell note | under 90 characters, one sentence or two short ones |
| Beat card, camp story, Bond story, Lore page | 2-5 sentences, one tap |
| Anything else | not a wall of text: split it or cut it |

## 2. The world

### 2.1 The land and the road

The land is called **Lanternfall**. It is a country of hollows, woods, barrows and marsh inland, a
grey coast to the west, mountains to the north, and a wide green plain in the east called **the
Lea**, which has burned since the Fall and is now **the Emberwaste**.

**The Lantern Road** runs through all of it, lamp to lamp. The old Lantern Order built it so a
traveller was never out of sight of a light. It loops: it leaves the Hollow, runs down to the coast,
crosses to the Lea, climbs to the mountain pass and comes back round. (The loop matters at the end,
8.6. The Lantern Road strip in the Camp view is a straight line for now; a later map can bend it.)

Each region along the road has one **Great Lantern**, the big lamp that the region's small lamps
were lit from. Great Lanterns are the chapter ends of the story.

### 2.2 Lantern light and fire

There are two kinds of light, and the whole story rests on the difference.

- **Fire** is ordinary. Campfires, candles, Pip's spells, a forge. Fire is warm, but the dark is
  not afraid of it. Deep enough, fire burns blue and the dark stops moving away (Deep Lore page 4).
- **Lantern light** is the **Old Light**: a light older than fire that lies deep under the land
  (Deep Lore page 8, "What Glows"). Long ago, small folk cut a stair down to it (page 3, the stair
  "cut by hands smaller than ours") and carried it up in lamps. Every lantern of the Order was lit
  from that first flame, lamp to lamp. The dark steps back from lantern light.

Player-facing, keep it simple: "Fire keeps you warm. Lantern light keeps the dark back."

### 2.3 The rules of light (for writers; players learn them one at a time)

1. **The dark fears lantern light, and it wants it.** Like a cold hand near a fire.
2. **The Voice can call a lantern light out of its lamp**, anywhere it has something listening for
   it. A called light rises and flies to it. A lamp it has called will not catch again with fire.
3. **A light lit for someone cannot be called.** A lamp lit for one person, living or dead, is
   given, and a given light belongs to them. A lamp lit from a given light is given too.
4. **Stolen light is held still.** The Voice keeps what it takes and never lets it go out. Held
   light burns cold green (the Lurelight, Saltreach's drowned lamps, the Lanternjellies) or angry
   red (the Emberwaste, the Ashen Wyrm). It never goes out, and it warms no one.

The Voice's one promise to everyone is rule 4 dressed up: "your light will never go out."
The Lampwardens' Oath says "Hold the road. Keep the light. Give it to no one." The Order meant
"never hand it to the dark". The story's lesson is gentler and stranger: the safest light is the
one you give to someone.

### 2.4 The Lantern Order and its four callings

The **Lantern Order** kept the road for centuries. Its knights and keepers swore the Oath (above)
and lit the lamps from the chapel on the hill above the Hollow, where the Order kept its first
lamp, the **Mother Lamp**. In long peaceful years the Order faded "knight by knight, into farms and
taverns and quieter lives" (Aldric). By the Fall only lampwardens like Maren and Silas, a chapel
bellringer (Anselm), one old knight (Aldric) and the Saint (Elowen) were left.

The Order had four callings. They are the four hero classes:

| Calling | Class | What it meant | The Oath words it lived by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wardens** hold | Warden | They stood between the lamp and the dark, so the dark hit them first | "Hold the road." |
| **Rangers** walk | Ranger | Scouts who walked the dark stretches between lamps and found the weak spot in what waited there | "Hold the road" (by knowing it) |
| **Lanternmages** burn | Lanternmage | They spent a little lantern light as flame and threw it. The Order distrusted them, so most were hedge-taught from old books like Pip's | "Keep the light" (by spending it well) |
| **Lightkeepers** keep | Lightkeeper | The tenders: they kept lamps, and people, burning. Elowen was one | "Keep the light." |

Short form for copy: **Wardens hold. Rangers walk. Lanternmages burn. Lightkeepers keep.**

In the Hollow, common folk call anyone who carries a living lamp "a lightkeeper", whatever they do
with it. That is why Bram says "Heard a Lightkeeper was on the road" to a hero of any class.

## 3. The Fall: why the world is dark

### 3.1 What happened (the truth, for writers)

- **Before the Fall.** Something in the far dark had been listening for a long time. It called
  small lights first: green wisps over the Wraithmarsh that people followed into the water (that is
  how Thessaly's village drowned, "long ago", before the marsh rose). It spoke to the Hollow King's
  court behind a curtain. Nobody put the pieces together.
- **The night of the Fall, about ten years before the game.** The Voice sang. Every lantern light
  in the land rose out of its lamp at once and streamed east over the hills, like sparks up a
  chimney. From far away it looked like a rain of falling stars coming down on the Lea. The Lea
  caught fire where the lights landed, and it has burned red ever since: the Emberwaste. That same
  night the fire grew wings (the young Ashen Wyrm) and fell on Emberlea, and Caedmon held the road
  out for one hour.
- **What held.** Lamps lit for someone did not rise (rule 3): the Barrow Lamp, lit for the dead,
  "flickered and held" (Maren). Hesketh's lamp on the hill, lit the night his wife died, "did not go
  out even when all the others did". From the coast, Silas saw it: "far inland one small light
  burned on a hill". Maud's Lantern at the bottom of the Deepwell, lit for the lost miners, burns
  still.
- **What Elowen did.** She was in the chapel on the hill, holding the Mother Lamp, "the last one
  still burning". It pulled at her hand. She could not hold it for everyone; it would have gone
  too. So she made her choice: she broke its flame into small sparks and put each one into the
  hands of someone running past in the dark, lit *for* them. Given away, none could be called.
  She kept one spark for herself and turned it so low the Voice could not hear it. Then she stayed
  in the dark and waited. It hurts because she chose to scatter the land's light among strangers
  and leave the land dark for years, not knowing if anyone would ever bring it back. "I did not
  think anyone would come back here." She would make the choice again.
- **What people believe.** The road songs say the Saint let the last lantern fall from the chapel
  tower and every lamp fell dark with it: the Lanternfall. They are half right about the name and
  wrong about the rest. "The lanterns did not fall," she says. They were called, and she kept one
  back by giving it away.

### 3.2 What the dark is

The dark is not night. Night still comes and goes; the camp has a sunrise. The dark is a thickness
in the world, like smoke you cannot see, that sits in low places (caves, marsh, barrows, deep
water, under the ground) and spreads out at dusk. Where it lies thick, days are a long grey dusk
("The dusk has not ended," Anselm). It is cold, it is patient, and it moves. It is older than the
first lantern. A lit lantern thins it. A relit region gets its mornings back.

The dark is not a person. The **Voice** is the one thing in it that speaks (8.6 says what it is).

### 3.3 Why the lamps stay dark

Since the Fall, a lamp that was lit from the Order's chain will not catch with fire. Hesketh "kept
walking and kept lighting, and none of them caught". Silas "lit it again and again, and it would
not catch." Two reasons, and the player learns both:

1. Its light was called away, and fire is not lantern light (2.2).
2. While something in a region still listens for the Voice, any new lantern light there would be
   called too. Each region has one such **Listener**. Its region boss is that Listener (4.4).

Your lamp is different: it is one of Elowen's given sparks (5.1). A lamp lit from it is given too,
so it holds (rule 3). Beat a region's Listener, set your flame in its Great Lantern, and the whole
region can hold light again.

## 4. The monsters: what they are and why we fight them

### 4.1 The rule

- **The dark soaks into things that stay in it too long** (Deep Lore page 7: "what the dark makes of
  things that stay below too long"). Moss, bats, beetles, stone, the dead, the drowned. It does not
  make monsters out of nothing. Every foe was part of the land first.
- **The dark in them aches for light.** They come for your lamp the way moths come to a flame, but
  angrier. That is why a moving lamp on a dark road draws a pack every few steps, and why they
  always come to you.
- **Beaten, the dark goes out of them.** Say it in the bestiary, never in gore: "Beaten, it is only
  moss again." The dead lie still. A golem is a pile of stone. A Lanternjelly's stolen light bursts
  free (that is its Jellylight buff).
- **They keep coming** because the dark keeps seeping in off the road, out of caves and marsh and
  sea, until a region is relit, and then more slowly after. This is why zones refill and why
  farming a cleared zone makes sense.
- **Elders wear crowns.** A thing that has held the dark longest grows bigger and wears a crown
  (the elder art already has a gold band and a red gem). The Voice makes kings of what listens
  longest. The Hollow King's court is the same idea on a grand scale. Killing crowned things is
  why Corvin's route is called Kingslayer.
- **Champions** are the strongest of a kind, drawn from far off by your light. They carry its best
  parts home with them (Trophies).

## 5. The hero

### 5.1 Who you are

You grew up in **Mossy Hollow**, a hedge village at the edge of the Hollow. For ten years one small
lamp hung over a door there and never went out, and the village lived inside its little circle of
light. Nobody knew why it held. (It was one of Elowen's sparks, lit for you when you were small. You
do not remember who handed it to your family. Elowen does. That is her thread, section 6.)

The night the game begins, the dark finally came into Mossy Hollow. The bats came down on the road,
and the village ran (Tobin's story). You took the lamp off its hook and carried it out. Tobin took
your spare sword. Bram left too. Behind you, Mossy Hollow went dark.

You are not chosen. You are the one who picked the lamp up. The character screen already says it:
"You carry the last lantern."

### 5.2 Why you relight the lanterns

1. **Because yours catches.** Every other lamp on the road is dead. Your flame lights them, and
   what you light holds (rule 3). You find this out in the first minutes: the cold Hearth catches
   from your lamp when Hesketh's would not.
2. **Because the dark is coming for everyone else.** Mossy Hollow was the last lit village you
   knew. Each lamp you light makes a stretch of road safe to walk, and people come back to it
   (the Hands, the Tavern visitors, letters from Tobin's mother).
3. **Because every relit region gets its mornings back.** The monsters thin out, the camp grows,
   and the next stretch of road opens.
4. **Because something took the light, and it is still out there.** That becomes the reason once
   the Coast is lit.

### 5.3 Other lamp-bearers (the online layer)

The Tavern shows other heroes, and the raid is fought by everyone. Story: Elowen gave away many
sparks, down every road. Other bearers carry theirs. Each one's story is their own, and each one
also thinks theirs is the last lantern. When the war horn sounds, lights from other roads answer.
This needs no change to the online layer; it is how writers talk about it.

### 5.4 The class in the story

The class is the calling you took up with the lamp (2.4). It changes how you fight, not who you
are. Class-specific lines live in hero Bonds (formation.md 2.3) and nowhere else, so the main story
reads the same for every class.

### 6.3 Bonds in the story

Bonds grow when two people fight side by side (formation.md 2.3). In the story, a Bond is two
people learning each other in the dark, and it matters for a reason beyond comfort: **a light lit
for someone cannot be stolen** (rule 3). When a Bond reaches **Sworn**, each of the pair could carry
a flame lit for the other. That is what **Lanternborn** means (rank 7, and the companion endgame D5
builds on Sworn Bonds): a companion who can carry a light of their own, given by a friend.
**Lanternlit** (rank 8, after the Coast) is the next step: the flame burns in them, fed by the freed
light in Lantern Pearls.

Bond story guidance for LORE7: story 1 (Friends) is a small shared moment; story 2 (Close) is a
secret one tells the other; the Sworn line is one sentence of trust, spoken out loud. Keep the 21
titles in formation.md; they are canon now.

## 7. The Hearth, Hollow's Rest and the Hands

### 7.1 Why a camp

Hollow's Rest was a mining camp over an old well, then a village, then empty. The miners "dug for
silver and found a stair instead" (Deep Lore page 2). It sits right above the Old Light (4.5), so a
fire lit there from your lamp burns strong and steady. It is the one place the dark never comes.
Hesketh chose it for his fire for that reason, though he only says: "Every road needs a place to
come back to."

The **Hearth** grows from a campfire to a hearth to the **Lantern Hall** (camp.md). Each level is
the town remembering it is a town. The Hearth 10 title "Keeper of Hollow's Rest" means it.

Stations are built because a lit camp needs tools before swords: a Workbench, then a Forge, then a
Storehouse, and so on. The cold start (hearth-and-hands.md 1) is the first scene of the story.

### 7.2 Who the Hands are

The Hands are the people of the Hollow who hid for ten years: in cellars, in the quarry town's
tunnels, in hedge villages with one candle. They are not fighters. Word travels that there is a fire
on the road that holds, and they come. They gather because a town needs wood and ore. They follow
you because your fire is the first safe place they have seen in years, and because it is warm.

- **Tam**, Hesketh's nephew, comes first (he heard from his uncle).
- **Nan Tarrow** worked the quarry before the dark (Grenna knows her).
- **Old Bracken** felled the Hollow woods with Bram's father.
- **Sister Fennel** kept the herb garden at Elowen's chapel.
- **Jory Quickhands** lived by his wits in the dark and will not say how.
- **Mother Ashby** is from Emberlea. Hers was one of the families Caedmon got out. She keeps a place
  at the camp table for him, too.

Voice for Hands: ordinary, practical, a bit tired, glad of the fire. They talk about work, weather,
food and each other. They never explain the lore; they only notice things ("The lamps held all
night again.").

### 7.3 Day and night

Hollow's Rest has real mornings because the Hearth burns (3.2). At dusk the lanterns come on one by
one, and everyone comes to the fire. Night at camp is safe, never scary.

### 8.2 Chapter 2: the Sunken Coast (zones 36-70, about weeks 1-4)

**Question:** where did the light go?

Already written (`21b-stories-coast.js`, region-2.md 1): Hallam, the tide that "is not the moon

### 8.3 Chapter 3: the Emberwaste (zones 71-105; spec D4, proposal)

**Question:** can the stolen light be taken back?

This is story input for the D4 spec. D4 owns the names, foes and numbers; this section says what the
region is for.

- **The place.** The Lea was green farmland. The stolen lights fell there and burn still, held
  still (rule 4), so the ground is ash, glass and ember. The Ashen Wyrm roosts here. Emberlea, the
  village Caedmon saved, stood on its western edge; its people rebuilt far away.
- **Suggested zone types (seven places, each a foe that tests one thing):** Cinder Road (Cinder
  Hounds, sparks with legs that run in packs), Emberlea Ruins (Ashwalkers: the ash walks the empty
  streets in the shapes of the families who got out; nobody died here, and that matters), the
  Ashfall (Ash Moths, drawn to held light), the Glass Flats (Glasswalkers: sand fused by falling
  light, standing up), the Kilns (Slagbacks), Wyrmscale Ridge (Wyrmlings, the Wyrm's brood), and the
  Pyre (Kept Lights: stolen lantern lights with legs, still in the shape of their lamps).
- **The Listener: the Pyre Knight** (name for D4 to confirm). A knight of the Order who stood with
  Caedmon on the Fall night. The voice in the fire made them both an offer: keep this fire, and it
  never goes out. Caedmon did not agree. This knight did, and has kept the pyre ever since.
  Caedmon, if fielded: "He agreed. I did not."
- **Pip's last chapter.** Somewhere in the Emberwaste lies the rest of Pip's book (or the
  Lanternmage hero carries it: Bond "The Last Page"). The torn line ends: "To put a fire out, you
  must **give it somewhere to go**." That is how you win the region: not by smothering the fire, but
  by giving the stolen lights their way home.
- **Chapter end: the Great Lantern of the Emberwaste** (the Emberlea lamp tower in the middle of
  the Pyre). You set your flame in it, and it is a given light, so the held lights around it
  remember they were lit for someone once. Thousands of small lights lift off the Lea and fly home
  over the hills, west, to every dark lamp they left. Back in the Hollow and on the Coast, the lamps
  brighten. The ash is warm, and something green is growing. Caedmon goes to sit at a table.
- **The turn.** As the pyre goes out, one line, from the fire itself: **"It was never here. Down.
  It was always down."** The Voice was never in the Emberwaste. The Emberwaste was where it kept
  what it took. The Voice card's "it came from inland" was only an echo off the hoard.

### 8.6 The ending (sealed: coordinator, owner and end-game writers only)

**Who the Voice is.** Long ago the small folk carried the Old Light up the stair in the first
lantern. Every lantern casts a shadow. The first one cast the first. It was left at the bottom of
the stair, in the cold, while the light went up into the world and made everything warm. It has
wanted to be warm ever since. It does not know how to tend a light, only how to take one, and a
taken light goes still and cold (rule 4). So it takes more. That is the Voice: **the first shadow**,
the cold the first lantern left behind. It is not evil. It is lonely, and it is very old.

**Where it is.** At the bottom of the Deepwell, under Hollow's Rest, below the Old Light's spring,
where the stair ends. The end of the Lantern Road is the place you came back to every night.

**How it ends.** You cannot put out a shadow, and killing is not the answer here. At the end of the
stair you do what Elowen did: you light a lamp for someone. You light one for it. A given light
cannot be taken, so for the first time it holds a light it did not steal. It learns that a light
has to be tended, lit again each dusk, or it goes out. The dark steps back and is only night again.

Thessaly's secret: the one holding the lantern high on the dark road, with the dark stepping back,
is the Voice itself, holding the lamp you gave it. Anselm rings the last toll: the last dusk of the
long dusk. Vesper finishes her verse. Oriel's stars stop sending bad news and go back to gossip.

**Why this ending suits the game.** No resets, nothing lost, nothing destroyed: the story ends in
giving, like the game's rule that you never lose what you earned. The world goes on after it: the
lamps still need lighting every night, so there is always a reason to play.

The last line of the game, spoken by Hesketh at the fire: "Every road needs a place to come back
to." The player has now heard it twice, and it means more the second time.

## 11. Canon kept straight: what this bible reinterprets

Nothing already written is changed. These readings are canon now:

   sparks (3.1). Every bearer carries one of the last lanterns; each thinks theirs is the last (5.3).

   any lamp-bearer a lightkeeper (2.4).

5. **Silas saw "one small light on a hill" far inland.** It was Hesketh's lamp on the hill above the
   Hollow, lit for his wife.
6. **Thessaly's village drowned "long ago", before the marsh rose**, yet the dark came at the Fall.
   The Voice called small lights (the green wisps) years before it sang (3.1).
7. **The raid's Hollow King drops the Crown of Hollows, but the pinnacle says "the crown was not on
   him".** The Crown of Hollows is his road crown. The iron crown stays behind the curtain (4.7).
8. **The Ashen Wyrm is a "young wyrm" in the First Fire and a raid boss now.** It was born from the
   falling light on the Fall night, ten years ago, and has grown. It keeps coming back because the
   Emberwaste builds it again (4.7).
9. **Deep Lore "What Glows" (an old light the dark fears, waiting) and the Climber (climbing toward
   the light).** The Old Light's spring is at the last landing; Maud hung her lantern over it; the
   Climber comes from further down (4.5).
10. **"Morwen is a Tallow" (pinnacle writing).** She is Maud's great-niece (6.2).
11. **Anselm's chapel and Elowen's chapel.** They are the same chapel on the hill. He rang its dusk
    bell; it cracked on the Fall night; he carried the bell out, and she stayed (new, fits both).
12. **Isolde's contract, "the other hand was shaking", and formation.md's "Corvin's hand signed her

13. **The Hollow's region boss is "the Elder of Wraithmarsh V, as always".** It is the Listener; it
    can take that display name (4.2).
14. **The PIN_VOICE card says the Voice came from the Emberwaste.** True as far as anyone knew. In

## Appendix A: existing canon (audit, 2026-09-28)

Facts already in the game or its specs. This bible contradicts none of them.

### A.1 World, places and names

- The land is called Lanternfall (`21-stories.js` Elowen bio; legacy `COMPS` Lantern Saint blurb:
  "The reason this land is called Lanternfall").
- Regions (`22-data-regions.js`): the Hollow (zones 1-35), the Sunken Coast (36-70), then the
  Emberwaste (the dark lantern past the road's end). Great Lanterns: "The Great Lantern of the
  Hollow", "The Great Lantern of the Coast".
- Hollow zones (`20-data.js`): Mossy Hollow, Batwing Caves, The Bonefield, Beetle Barrows, Fungal
  Deep, Quarry Ruins, Wraithmarsh. Placeholder coast names exist (Seamoss Hollow, Sea Caves...).
- Coast places (region-2.md): Grey Shingle, Gullcliffs, The Wrecks, Kelp Shallows, Glimmer Lagoon,
  Drowned Saltreach, The Coral Nave; boss zone Saltreach Light.
- Hollow's Rest was a mining camp before it was a village; the miners found a stair (Deep Lore).
  It starts as Old Hesketh's campfire "in a hollow off the road" (camp.md).
- The Hearth grows Campfire, Hearth, Lantern Hall; Hearth 10 title "Keeper of Hollow's Rest".
- Emberlea: a village with one road out, saved by Caedmon; its people rebuilt far from the Wyrm.
- The Lantern Road strip, "Beyond: the Emberwaste"; the Lantern Road is the long-term vision's
  name for the regions.

### A.2 The night the lights went out

- Every lamp in the land went dark at once (Elowen, Hesketh, Silas).
- Hesketh was halfway down his route; every lamp behind him died at once; none caught again.
- Silas: every lamp on the coast went out at once; his would not catch; far inland one small light
  on a hill did not go out.
- The Barrow Lamp flickered and held; Maren thinks Elowen saved it.
- Hesketh's lamp on the hill above the Hollow, lit the night his wife died, did not go out.
- Elowen was in the chapel holding the last lamp still burning; she made a choice with it; she would
  make it again; it still hurts. "The lanterns did not fall." People named the land for what she did
  and got the story wrong.
- Saltreach drowned that night; the water came up the street "slow and sure"; Hallam rowed the
  children out; his ferry was lost.
- Since then the tide comes in twice an hour: "It is not the moon pulling it now."
- Oriel: the stars have sent bad news since the lights went out; before, they gossiped. A new star
  appeared the night you took up the road.
- Anselm: "The dusk has not ended." The chapel fell; he rang until the tower cracked, then carried
  the bell (Patience, cast from a village's melted spoons) away.
- Mossy Hollow went dark; Tobin and Bram both left it that night (formation.md Bond "Mossy Hollow").
  Bats came down on the Hollow road and the village ran (Tobin).

### A.3 The Voice and the great foes

- The voice under the water promised Silas his light would never go out if he gave it to the sea;
  he carried the lens down; the lighthouse burned green; ships followed it onto the reef.
- Caedmon heard a voice in the Wyrm's fire that knew his name; he walked out because he did not
  agree; three days in the flame; no pain after the first hour, only light.
- The Hollow King spoke through a curtain; Corvin served twenty years without seeing his face; when
  the King fell, the court knelt to whoever came next; something came next.
- PIN_VOICE: the Hollow court knelt to it; Silas heard it under the water; Caedmon heard it in the
  fire; it called the Climber up the stair for a thousand years; it came from inland, where the
  Emberwaste burns.
- Pinnacles: the Hollow King (empty coat; the crown is cold iron), the Lurelight (a green light on
  the reef that was never a lamp), the First Fire (Emberlea road, the young Wyrm, one hour), the
  Climber (a thousand years of stairs; Maud's Lantern).
- World raid bosses rotate: The Ashen Wyrm, The Hollow King, The Mire Colossus, The Glass Hydra,
  The Lantern Eater, The Pale Tyrant. Their uniques: Wyrmscale Helm, Crown of Hollows, Colossus
  Pick, Hydra Glass, Lantern Eater's Fang, Pale Tyrant's Axe. Raid rewards are Embers.

### A.4 The Deepwell (Deep Lore pages)

The Rope; the Diggers (miners dug for silver, found a stair); the Stair (cut by smaller hands, worn
in the middle as if something climbed it for a thousand years); the First Lantern (flames turned
blue below the ninth landing and the dark stopped moving away); the Quiet Landings (every sixth
landing still, benches, oil, "Maud"); Maud (kept the camp lamps, went down after the miners); the
Elders (what the dark makes of things that stay below too long); What Glows (older than fire, what
the dark is afraid of, waiting); Maud's Lantern (never runs out); Why It Glows (she stayed so the
dark stays down there). Pinnacles: Maud has kept it down for a hundred years.

### A.5 The Oath and the Order

- The lantern order faded knight by knight; Aldric locked the chapter house and kept the key. His
  banner: a lantern on a crimson field.
- The Lampwardens' Oath, carved over the Coral Nave door: "Hold the road. Keep the light. Give it to
  no one." Someone scratched a line through the last words.
- Silas Penrow was a lampwarden of the Oath at Saltreach Light (206 stairs, "two hundred steps" in
  his barks). Old Hallam, the ferryman with no ferry, kept a tide book for thirty years.
- Circles (party-and-classes.md): Hedgefolk (common folk of the hollows), the Oath (the old lantern
  order), Dusk Company (sellswords who work at night), Wayfarers (travellers passing through).
- Oaths mode: the old lampwardens swore oaths to hold their roads against worse; you swear them again.
