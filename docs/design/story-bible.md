# Story bible: Lanternfall, Season 1

Status: **v2**, 2026-10-05 (thread "Story overhaul"). v1 was red-teamed (3.2 against a 4.3 bar); v2 rebuilds the rules,
gives the hero real choices, adds the Order's Oath twist, a person in every area, and a story role for every Captain,
Champion and Elder. Review rounds are logged in section 15.

Cal's notes this answers (2026-10-05): "Our story is really quite weak at the moment. Some of the popups that give
context of the world don't even make sense ... we should have a setup where the story is built and reviewed until we
have some serious depth and excitement added to each section of the game. It should all build to the end of area 5
where we fight The Voice ... there should still be a satisfying conclusion but ... leave space for the story to
continue after." Then: "we should include NPCs that either appear once or becoming recurring characters in the story"
and "it's also important to consider how the champions/captains/elders of darkness fit into the story as well."

"Area 5" is the fifth region, the Gloamvale, where the Voice is the Elder of Darkness. This file calls the five regions
**chapters** so they never get confused with the 35 areas inside them.

**This file is the single source of truth for the story.** It replaces `lore.md` wherever they disagree and adopts the
delivery system of `story-c28.md` (its section 4) with the changes in section 10 here. The audit behind it is
[`story-audit-2026-10-05.md`](story-audit-2026-10-05.md). The depth bar is [`docs/review/story.md`](../review/story.md).
How the story keeps being built and reviewed is section 13.

**Binding facts** (owner decisions in `docs/DECISIONS.md`): 5 regions x 7 areas x 5 zones; each zone is 5 fights then
its Shadowborn Captain; each area ends with a Champion of Darkness, each region with an Elder of Darkness. Enemies come
from the dark; they are not corrupted creatures. Hunting beasts are ordinary animals the dark drove into a rage. One hero
fights one foe at a time; there is no party. Region bosses are agents of the dark, never lamp-keepers. The hero is the
child Elowen's spark was lit for; the one who carried them stays unnamed. Season 1 ends with the first fight against the
Voice: the hero wins, the Voice retreats into the Deepwell, and a reveal sets up Season 2. Owner-chosen names are never
replaced. Art is still images and icons, no animation.

## 1. The story in one breath

Ten years ago a Voice in the dark sang, and every lamp in the land went out, except the few that were lit for someone
else. You grew up under one of them. Tonight the dark came up through the moss for it, so you took it and ran, to lead
the dark away from everyone you love. On the old Lantern Road you relight the land region by region, and learn two
things: your lamp was lit for you, and a light only goes out when it is alone. At the end of the road, in a valley where
the sky has been shut for a thousand years, the Voice gets you alone and puts your lamp out. Every lamp you lit for
someone on the way answers, and you drive it back into the ground.

**Theme, in one line:** *Every flame goes out. That is why you light one for someone.* The Voice says the first half
all game. The hero says the second half at the end.

**Central question** (asked on the first night, answered in the last fight): *Why does my light still burn, and what is
it for?* Chapter 1 answers "why" (it was lit for you). Chapters 2 to 4 show what keeping a light alone costs. Chapter 5
answers "what for": giving, and what you give comes back.

## 2. Tone and voice

- Warm, hopeful, a little melancholy. People have lost things and talk about it quietly. They are still kind and a bit
  funny, and they still light the fire every night.
- Short sentences. Active voice. Plain words. Name things the way a player would. Speech is short and sounds like a
  person, never a prophecy.
- Show one small true thing. Leave one thing unsaid.
- No gore, no cruelty for fun, no children harmed on the page, no despair without a hand held out.
- The dark is "the dark". Before the player has earned the name, the Voice is "a voice", "something", "it".
- **The Voice is never a cartoon.** It is calm and partly right. Every flame does go out. Each offer is what a tired or
  frightened person wants to hear. The player should feel the pull before the story shows the price.
- **Some losses stay lost** (section 9.3). The Voice's claim has to be true sometimes, or the answer means nothing.
- **One small delight a chapter**, with no plot: a fishing-song contest, Caedmon trying Ashby's soup, Kestrel learning
  to pass a candle, a lamb in the Gloamvale. Camp lines carry them.

## 3. The world and its rules

### 3.1 The land

**Lanternfall**: hollows, woods, barrows and marsh in the middle; a grey coast to the west; the Lea, a green plain to
the east that has burned since the Fall (the **Emberwaste**); mountains to the north (the **Pale Reach**); and past the
last pass a valley nobody on the road has seen (the **Gloamvale**).

**The Lantern Road** runs through all of it, lamp to lamp. The **Lantern Order** built it so a traveller was never out
of sight of a light. Each region has one **Great Lantern**; each area has a **waymark**, the Order's road lamp for that
stretch. **Lantern Hill** stands over **Hollow's Rest**, the hero's camp. On it are the dark Great Lantern of the
Hollow, the Order's chapel (where Elowen hides) and one small lamp Hesketh lit for his wife the night she died. Under
Hollow's Rest is the **Deepwell**, a stair cut long ago down toward the **Old Light**, where Maud's Lantern hangs.

### 3.2 The rules (for writers; players learn them one at a time)

| # | Rule | Player hears it from | Shown by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Fire is not lantern light.** Fire keeps you warm. Lantern light, the Old Light from under the land, keeps the dark back. | Hesketh, opening | His dead lamps that fire won't relight |
| 2 | **Kept light can be called. Given light cannot.** A light is *given* when it is lit for another person's sake. A light kept for yourself, or for no one, or as a duty to whoever passes, is *kept*, and the Voice's song can call it out of its lamp. A lamp lit from a given flame, for someone, is given too. | Elowen, end of Ch1 | The Lanternfall night; the Order's road lamps; Maren's lamp; yours |
| 3 | **A given light goes out only when it is alone.** The dark can smother a given light up close, but only when no other given light lit from it or for it is still burning. Lights in company hold each other. | Maren, without words (Ch1 a4); said plainly by Elowen | Mossy Hollow's ten years; Hesketh's first fire; the Long Night; the finale |
| 4 | **The dark has no shape of its own.** What climbs out of it copies shapes it finds: the place, the things in it, and the shapes in people's memory. It copies shapes, never voices. | Anselm (Ch1 a3) | Ossuary Marshal; Emberlea's ash; Rowan's shape |
| 5 | **The dark has no voice of its own.** It speaks only with voices given to it by people who said yes to one of its offers. A yes costs your voice. The Voice lends held voices to its Elders and to some Champions. When the one holding a voice is beaten, a living person's voice goes home to them; a dead person's voice goes to rest. | Morwen (hint, Ch1 a5); Silas (Ch2); seen in full Ch4 a5 | The Oracle; the Fogbound; the Silent Village |
| 6 | **Held light cannot be destroyed, only held.** On the Lanternfall night every called light flew toward the Lea; a few fell short and were caught on the way (in the marsh, in the lagoon). Freed, a light flies back to the lamp it left. Held a long time, it forgets the way, and has to be given a road. | Ch1 end (a few), Ch2 a5, Ch3 (thousands) | |

Rules 2 and 3 are the heart. Rule 3 answers every "why didn't the dark just…?": your lamp held ten years because a whole
village lived inside its light; it is safe on the road because every lamp you relight is lit from it and stays lit
behind you; the Voice can only beat you by getting you alone, which is what Chapter 5 is.

### 3.3 The Lantern Order and its Oath (the hidden twist)

The Order's Oath was **"Hold the road. Keep the light. Give it to no one."** For centuries it was a vow of duty: the
Order kept every road lamp, the waymarks and the Great Lanterns, for whoever passed. No lamp was lit for anyone. **That
is why the Voice could take the whole road in one night**: every Order lamp was kept, and kept light can be called.
Elowen broke the Oath. She gave the Mother Lamp away in sparks, each lit for a stranger, and that is the only reason any
light survived. The road songs say the Saint *let the lantern fall*. The Order's survivors call her an oathbreaker. Both
are true. The player learns this in Chapter 2 (section 8.2) and sees the road differently from then on: every waymark
you relight is the Oath being undone, one lamp at a time, because you light each one for someone.

### 3.4 What the dark is, and what Shadowborn are

The dark is not night. It is a cold thickness that sits in low places and spreads at dusk. It was here before the first
lamp, and it wants the land the way it was: no light anywhere. Where a lamp burns, it cannot be. Section 5 describes the
dark's ranks: the Voice, its Elders, Champions, Captains and Shadowborn.

### 3.5 The Lanternfall night (the truth, for writers)

- For centuries the Voice gathered small yeses: travellers who sat down in the marsh fog to rest, a king's court behind a
  curtain, keepers who were tired. Each yes gave it a voice. By the Fall it had a choir.
- **Ten years ago it sang**, to put the whole land out in one night. Every kept light rose out of its lamp and flew east.
  The Order's road lamps went first. They came down on the Lea like falling stars, and the Lea has burned with them since.
- **What held:** given lights. The Barrow Lamp (Maren lit it for the dead she buried). Hesketh's lamp on the hill (for his
  wife). And the sparks of the **Mother Lamp**: in the chapel, Elowen felt it pull at her hand, broke its flame and
  pressed each spark into the hands of someone running past in the dark, lit *for* them. She gave away about forty. One
  went into the hands of the one carrying you. You were very small.
- **After the Fall** the dark hunted the given lights the slow way: Shadowborn, one at a time, waiting for each to be
  alone. Most of Elowen's forty went out in the first years, carried away by people who fled alone. Yours was hung over a
  door in Mossy Hollow, and a whole village lived inside it. It held.

## 4. The hero

### 4.1 Who you are

You grew up in **Mossy Hollow**, a hedge village at the bottom of the Hollow, under a small lamp over your door that
never went out. You are the starter you pick, Wren, Tobin or Pip; the main story reads the same for all three, and each
hero has a line of their own at every big moment (4.5). Your family is never named; the village raised you.

### 4.2 The arc

| | |
|---|---|
| **Want** (Ch1) | Keep the dark away from the people you love. You do it by carrying the lamp away from them. |
| **Flaw** | **"I carry it so nobody else has to."** You take the danger on alone and call it a gift. |
| **Need** | Let others carry light too. A light alone goes out; a light given and shared holds. |
| **Tested** | Ch1: running alone with the lamp is what nearly gets it smothered; Hesketh's fire saves it. Ch2: Hallam asks to carry the lens up the two hundred steps, and you let him (a first). Ch3: Caedmon goes into the Pyre first; you let him. Ch4: the Reach lights your lamp for you, and you let them. Ch5: alone again, and the lamp goes out. |
| **Final answer** | The Voice: "Every flame goes out." The hero lights a taper from the returning light, in its face: "Then we light another." |

### 4.3 The hero's choices

An idle RPG with no dialogue tree still needs decisions that cost something and pay off later. Three, all cheap in data:

1. **"Lit for" at each Great Lantern** (Ch1, Ch2, Ch3). When you set your flame in a Great Lantern you choose who it is
   lit for, from the people you have met in that chapter (for example Hesketh, Tam or Bram in the Hollow; Hallam, Maren or
   Nell on the Coast). The name goes on the Great Lantern card and its Codex page. In the finale, those lamps answer
   first, by name. Different saves, different names.
2. **The Reach reverses it** (Ch4). The Reach lights its new Great Lantern *for you*. You don't choose; you receive.
3. **Coldhearth** (Ch5 a5). Your lamp is guttering, alone. You can keep all your flame, or light one cold hearth in
   Coldhearth from it (and Morwen's candle, if you carry it). Lighting it costs you: the lamp burns low for the rest of the
   chapter (a still, a dimmer lamp icon, no stat change). If you lit it, in the finale the very first light to answer is
   that hearth, a hundred years cold.

None of these change power, rewards or progress. They change words and stills.

### 4.4 Heroes, the lamp and the roster

- **The lamp passes to whoever you play** (owner decision). In the story the starters you didn't pick are people you meet
  in Chapter 1 (Tobin in area 1, Wren in area 2, Pip in area 3), and switching hero is handing them the lamp. A lamp handed
  to a friend is given, so it can't be called (rule 2). The camp line on a switch says so.
- **Other heroes carry their own sparks.** Elowen gave away about forty; a few were tended. Heroes from the 32-hero roster
  who appear in the story (Elowen, Caedmon, Kestrel, Maren, Anselm, Morwen, Thessaly, Grenna, Hesketh, Bram, Vesper,
  Oriel, Asta, Isolde) **cannot be unlocked before their first scene** in this bible. Unlock routes must be gated on that
  scene (a note for the hero-roster cards).

## 5. The dark's ranks: how the enemies fit the story

Every enemy the player fights has a place in one chain of command and a reason to be where it is.

| Rank | What it is | Speaks? | Story job | Where the player meets its story |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Voice** | The dark that was here first, and the choir of every voice it was given | Yes, with borrowed voices only | Wants the land dark. Makes the offers. Sends the Elders. | Ch1 hints, Ch2 first words, Ch5 throughout, the finale |
| **Elders of Darkness** (5) | One a region. The dark set over that region, each wearing an offer the Voice made there | With lent voices | Keep a whole region dark: while one stands, no light there lasts the night unless it has company | Elder scenes; the region's journal; Vesper's verse |
| **Champions of Darkness** (35) | Dark-born lieutenants. Each rules one area for its Elder and does one job in the Elder's plan | A few, with voices their Elder lends them | Hold their area's waymark dark and do their job (tables below) | Area title, Champion pre and post scenes, journal page, unique flavour |
| **Shadowborn Captains** (175) | The strongest Shadowborn of a zone, marked and named | Never | Hold a seam open; each does one specific harm to that zone | The Captain's title and banner line before its first move |
| **Shadowborn** (175 kinds) | What climbs out of a seam, in a shape copied from the place | Never | Put out lights, yours first | The zone line on the walk-in; the Bestiary |

**Rules for writers.**
- **Zone lines** say why *this* monster is *here*: the shape it copied and the light it is after. They name the monster.
- **Captain lines** name the one harm this Captain does ("It holds the seam open"; "It swallowed the lamp over the well").
  The area's **fifth Captain** always points at the Champion ("The rest answer to the throne in the green").
- **Champion scenes** say what it does for its Elder; its **post** shows the light it was holding come back, and one
  line of the Elder answering ("Out in the marsh, the fog gets thicker."). That line makes the Elder a felt presence for
  the whole chapter.
- **Elders** are not Shadowborn (except the Whitehush, which is the storm's own walker). Each is the region's argument.
- **Bosses never repeat** (owner decision). The Voice's ranks are beaten once, in story order.

### 5.1 The Voice

The dark that was here first, and the one part of it that learned to speak, with voices people gave it. For a thousand
years it has gathered the voices of everyone who said yes, and that choir is what sang the song. It does not hate people;
it hardly sees them. It wants the land still and dark and quiet. Its one belief is plain and partly right: **every flame
goes out.**

**Its five offers.** Each is a way to be alone with your light, and each sells a different loneliness. The hero hears
each one once and answers with their own line.

| Ch | Offer | The loneliness it sells | Who said yes, and the price the player sees |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"Rest."** | Exhaustion: "You're tired. Nobody is coming." | Travellers who sat down in the marsh fog. Their voices fill the Fenmother. The dead ones never come back. |
| 2 | **"Forever."** | Duty: "You're the only keeper. Only you can hold it." | Silas Penrow, who kept the Saltreach light alone and wrote letters he never sent. It never went out. It burned green and drowned ships. |
| 3 | **"Safe."** | Fear of loss: "Don't share it. Sharing is how it's stolen." | Ser Durand, who wanted Emberlea's lights safe from the Wyrm. They were so safe no one could have them, and the Lea burned for ten years. |
| 4 | **"Yours."** | Fear of others: "Shut the door. They'd take it." | The Silent Village, one family at a time, in the storm. Their candles were called, then their voices taken. |
| 5 | **"Let go."** | Despair: "Everyone alone ends the same. Come to the end now." | Offered to you. Coldhearth shows the end it means. |

**How it rises.**

| Ch | What it does | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Its Shadowborn hunt the last lamps; it does not know why yours lasts. Its song sounds in the caves; its offer comes through the Oracle. When the Fenmother falls, it notices you. | "a voice", "the song". Elowen: "It heard you tonight." |
| 2 | Speaks through the Fogbound and the drowned. Says your name for the first time (Ch2 a6). Pulls the tide. | One Voice line at a6; the Elder scene. |
| 3 | **Strikes at your home.** Veyr's glass shows it where your fire is; the Long Night comes for Hollow's Rest. The camp holds without you. | "I know where your fire is." Caedmon names it "the Voice". |
| 4 | Sends the storm to shut every door; wears Rowan's shape against Kestrel; shows you what it is made of. | The Silent Village. |
| 5 | **Gets you alone.** Puts out the waymarks behind you one by one, shuts the sky, talks to you (five lines in the chapter, no more). Puts your lamp out. Loses. | Five Voice lines, then the finale. |

**Its end in Season 1.** Section 9.

## 6. The cast

Nobody joins your fights. People speak in the gaps between fights (section 10). Every area has at least one person in
it, and every recurring person changes. Section 6.4 lists every appearance.

### 6.1 Recurring characters

**Old Hesketh, the lamplighter** (camp; the mentor).
- *Want:* to light the road again. For ten years he walked his route and lit lamps that would not catch.
- *Cost:* he has not climbed Lantern Hill in ten years. He can't bear to look at his wife's lamp, the only one that held.
- *Arc:* opening: "Every lamp goes out. That's why you carry a spare wick." End of Ch1: he climbs the hill with you,
  lights his walking lamp from yours ("That's two"), and shows you his wife's lamp. Ch2 on: **the Lamp Rack**, a rack by
  the fire, one small lamp per area you clear, each lit by Hesketh from your flame and lit for someone; Hands carry them
  out to the waymarks. Ch3 Long Night: he stands at the rack with a taper, relighting each Hand's lamp as the dark puts it
  out. Finale: the rack's lamps are the road that answers. Epilogue: the last line.
- *Rule:* "Every road needs a place to come back to" is spent twice only: when the fire first catches, and at the end.

**Saint Elowen, the oathbreaker** (the chapel on Lantern Hill).
- *What she did:* broke the Oath and the Mother Lamp, and gave about forty sparks to strangers. One is your lamp.
- *Cost:* ten years alone in a dark chapel with one spark turned so low the Voice couldn't hear it, not knowing if any
  spark survived. The Order's survivors call her an oathbreaker.
- *Plants:* her mark, a small spiral, scratched on the base of your lamp (seen in the opening card's still), on the
  Barrow Lamp (Ch1 a4) and on Ada's candle (Ch1 a7). Her candle flares when the Chained Star falls (Ch1 a6).
- *Arc:* End of Ch1 she meets you: "You carried it all this way. I lit that for you. You were very small." She tells you
  rule 2 and rule 3, and opens the Proving. Ch2 end: she owns the Oath twist ("I broke it. I'd break it again."). One line at
  every later Great Lantern. Ch4 end: "Don't go alone." Finale: she turns her flame up to send it down the road.
  Epilogue: she keeps it up, on purpose, as bait (9.2).

**Vesper Lark, the road singer** (the Tavern).
- Sings "The Lantern Road": one verse per region, the last verse without an ending. It is what people believe, and it is

## 8. The road, chapter by chapter

Each chapter has a question, an offer, a midpoint turn, a low point, a climax and a hook. Each area has a **stake**, a
**turn** at its Champion and a **plant**. Turn types rotate: R reveal, V reversal, L loss, G gain, C choice, E escalation.
Zone and Captain lines (175 each) are the chapter writer cards' work, from the roster (`enemies-c22-*-final.md`) and
section 5. Chapter 1 is written closer to final text because Milestone 1 is "The Hollow, finished".

### 8.1 Chapter 1: The Hollow (zones 1 to 35). "The Last Lamp"

**Question:** why does my lamp still burn? **Offer:** "Rest." **Elder:** the Fenmother.
**Midpoint (a4):** a lamp alone nearly goes out; beside yours, it steadies. **Low point (a7):** the fog takes the road, and

## 9. The ending of Season 1

### 9.1 Victory

The Voice's last hands open. The light pours in, and the choir leaves it: the ten thousand from the Blind Mere, the living
ones home to their owners, the dead to rest. What's left is small. It speaks once more, in a voice no one has heard in this
story: not a monster's, not a choir. A small, tired, ordinary voice, like a person's. **"Every flame goes out. I can wait."**
The hero lights a taper from the returning light and holds it up in its face (a still): **"Then we light another."** (per
hero, 4.5). It sinks into the ground at the valley's heart and is gone, down.

### 9.2 The warm beat

One still and one line per card, a tap each, all skippable, never replayed.
1. The Seam opens wide. The sky over the Gloamvale is ordinary night. Real stars.
2. Across the land, at dawn, people wake up and speak: the living voices from the Mere, home. Doors open in villages you
   never saw.
3. Anselm rings Patience: the last toll. "That's the one I kept."
4. Elowen keeps her flame turned up. Hesketh doesn't like it. She: "It wants the light under the hill. Let it think I'm the
   one keeping it." (She is making herself bait, on purpose.)
5. Vesper finishes the song: "And the dark went down, and the lamps came on. / There. That's the ending. For now."
6. You walk back into Mossy Hollow. The hook over your door has been empty since the night you left. You light a new lamp
   from yours and hang it there. Tam is waiting. "This one's for whoever comes next."
7. Hesketh at the fire: "Every road needs a place to come back to."

### 9.3 Why this satisfies

- The central question is answered: the light was lit for you, and it was for giving.
- The hero's arc closes: the one who carried it alone is saved by every lamp they lit for someone, including the ones the
  player chose.
- Every recurring character pays off in the finale (Hesketh's rack, Elowen's flame, Anselm's toll, Vesper's verse, Thessaly's
  vision, Tam at the door).
- The Voice loses the fight plainly. Its claim is partly true and stays partly true: **Rowan, Thessaly's mother, Ashby's
  husband and Saltreach's drowned stay lost.** The answer is not "flames don't go out", it's "light another".
- Home, which the hero ran from on the first night, is lit again by their hand.

### 9.4 What stays true after

Night still comes and Shadowborn still climb out of old seams, so zones refill. Everything won stays won. The Voice is gone
from the Gloamvale. It is not gone.

## 11. Season 2: room to continue

### 11.1 What Season 1 closes

The land's lights are home. The Elders are beaten. The choir is broken up. The hero knows what their lamp is and what it is
for. The Oath is undone, one waymark at a time.

### 11.2 What Season 1 opens (new questions, not unfinished ones)

1. **Whose was that small, tired voice?** Someone said yes first, before the Order, and gave the dark its first voice. Who,
   and why did it go down the stair?
2. **Where did it go?** Into the Deepwell, under Hollow's Rest. The last still, after the credits card: at the bottom of the
   Deepwell, **Maud's Lantern flickers for the first time.** Planted by Grenna's "further than our ropes", the Deep Lore line
   "The stair goes further than any rope has measured", and Morwen's family name.
3. **Elowen is bait.** She turned her flame up so it would come to her. What is she planning, and is she right?
4. **The lullaby in the Mere.** One voice the hero almost knew. Never named. (The one who carried you stays unnamed; Season 2
   may never name them either.)
5. **The Order's survivors** want the Oath back. Aldric is torn.
6. **The dark's ranks are broken, not gone.** Shadowborn still climb out of old seams and the Captains are only
   driven down. The few Champions whose post scenes show them sinking rather than breaking (the Chained Star, Mora,
   Sevrin) are named in the Journal as "went down" and are Season 2's first faces. Bosses never repeat in Season 1.

Long-play players quit over an empty endgame (research, `fun-library.md` section 8), so these are concrete threads
with names on them, not a fog of "more to come". Recurring people each end Season 1 with one open line: Elowen (bait),
Aldric (the Oath), Grenna (the well cover), Morwen (Maud), Kestrel (where Rowan's voice went), Oriel ("one star is
moving under the ground").

### 11.4 Lore that unlocks in pieces

Each Champion beaten files one Journal page; each area cleared files its zone and Captain lines in the Bestiary; each
Elder files a verse of Vesper's song. A chapter's pages read as one story when complete, and the Journal shows the
gaps ("2 pages missing in the Hollow"). Locked things name what opens them in story terms ("Opens when the Hollow's
Elder falls"), sharing the hint pattern of the `ap-first-use-hints` card.

### 11.3 Rules Season 2 inherits

The six rules stay the rules. No resets. Tone stays warm. The Voice is never destroyed off screen.

## 13. How the story keeps being built and reviewed

### 13.1 The depth bar

[`docs/review/story.md`](../review/story.md) scores story work on twelve criteria. The bible and every chapter script must
reach **4 or more on every criterion and an average of 4.3 or more** from the Opus story judge. Everyday story copy uses the
`content` rubric plus the story rubric's hard checks.

### 13.2 The loop

1. **Write** (Sonnet 5.5 medium): a chapter script or story change, from this bible.
2. **Red team** (Sonnet 5.5 medium, argues against).
3. **Judge** (Opus 5.5 high, read-only): scores against `story.md`, rules on the red team, lists what a pass needs.
4. **Revise**, and repeat 2 and 3 until it passes, at most four rounds; after that the judge decides what ships and the rest
   becomes a follow-up card.
5. **Canon:** anything that changes this bible is a `judge` gate change, recorded in the PR and in `docs/DECISIONS.md` as
   "Claude decided", and reported in the digest for Cal's veto.

### 13.3 Standing checks

- **Weekly design review** (Opus high; `autopilot/playbook.md`): a story coherence check every week: the week's merged text
  against this bible and the ladder (12), the story check's output, and one finding about the weakest stretch of story still
  in the game.
- **`tools/check.mjs` "story"** (built by the delivery card): limits, required fields, every zone line names its monster, no
  retired words, no beat keyed to a companion, no text for content that isn't in the game, hero lines for all three starters,
  every choice has a default.
