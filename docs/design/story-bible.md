"Area 5" is the fifth region, the Gloamvale, where the Voice is the Elder of Darkness. This file calls the five regions
**chapters** so they never get confused with the 35 areas inside them.

**This file is the single source of truth for the story.** It replaces `lore.md` wherever they disagree and adopts the
delivery system of `story-c28.md` (its section 4) with the changes in section 10 here. The audit behind it is
[`story-audit-2026-10-05.md`](story-audit-2026-10-05.md). The depth bar is [`docs/review/story.md`](../review/story.md).

**Binding facts** (owner decisions in `docs/DECISIONS.md`): 5 regions x 7 areas x 5 zones; each zone is 5 fights then
its Shadowborn Captain; each area ends with a Champion of Darkness, each region with an Elder of Darkness. Enemies come
from the dark; they are not corrupted creatures. Hunting beasts are ordinary animals the dark drove into a rage. One hero
fights one foe at a time; there is no party. Region bosses are agents of the dark, never tied to lanterns or lamps (read in 5.2). The hero is the
child Elowen's spark was lit for; the one who carried them stays unnamed. Season 1 ends with the first fight against the
Voice: the hero wins, the Voice retreats into the Deepwell, and a reveal sets up Season 2. Owner-chosen names are never
replaced. Art is still images and icons, no animation.

## 1. The story in one breath

Ten years ago a Voice in the dark sang, and every lamp in the land went out, except the few that were lit for someone
else. You grew up under one of them. This winter the last lamp you could see from the village went out, and tonight the dark
came up through the moss for yours. So you took it and ran, to lead the dark away from everyone you love. On the old Lantern Road you
relight the land region by region, and learn two things: your lamp was lit for you, and a light only goes out when it is
alone. At the end of the road, in a valley whose sky the Voice shut two hundred years ago, it gets you alone and puts
your lamp out. Every lamp you lit for someone on the way answers, and you drive it back into the ground.

**Theme, in one line:** *Every flame goes out. That is why you light one for someone.* The Voice says the first half
all game. The hero says the second half at the end.

**Two questions, asked on the first night.** *Why does my light still burn?* Elowen answers it at the end of Chapter 1:
it was lit for you. *What is it for?* Chapters 2 to 4 show what keeping a light to yourself costs, and the last fight
answers it: giving, because what you give comes back.

## 2. Tone and voice

- Warm, hopeful, a little melancholy. People have lost things and talk about it quietly. They are still kind and a bit
  funny, and they still light the fire every night.
- Short sentences. Active voice. Plain words. Name things the way a player would. Speech is short and sounds like a
  person, never a prophecy.
- Show one small true thing. Leave one thing unsaid.
- No gore, no cruelty for fun, no children harmed on the page, no despair without a hand held out.
- The dark is "the dark". Before the player has earned the name, the Voice is "a voice", "something", "it".
- **The Voice is never a cartoon.** It is calm and partly right. Every flame does go out. Each offer is what a tired or
  frightened person wants to hear. The player should feel the pull before the story shows the price.
- **Some losses stay lost** (section 9.3). The Voice's claim has to be true sometimes, or the answer means nothing.
- **One small delight a chapter**, with no plot: a fishing-song contest, Caedmon trying Ashby's soup, Kestrel learning
  to pass a candle, a lamb in the Gloamvale. Camp lines carry them.

## 3. The world and its rules

### 3.1 The land

**Lanternfall**: hollows, woods, barrows and marsh in the middle; a grey coast to the west; the Lea, a green plain to
the east that has burned since the Fall (the **Emberwaste**); mountains to the north (the **Pale Reach**); and past the
last pass a valley nobody on the road has seen (the **Gloamvale**).

**The Lantern Road** runs through all of it, lamp to lamp. The **Lantern Order** built it so a traveller was never out
of sight of a light. Each region has one **Great Lantern**; each area has a **waymark**, the Order's road lamp for that
stretch. **Lantern Hill** stands over **Hollow's Rest**, the hero's camp. On it are the dark Great Lantern of the
Hollow, the Order's chapel (where Elowen hides) and one small lamp Hesketh lit for his wife the night she died, years before the Fall, from the Great Lantern itself. Under
Hollow's Rest is the **Deepwell**, a stair cut long ago down toward the **Old Light**, where Maud's Lantern hangs.

### 3.2 The rules (for writers; players learn them one at a time)

| # | Rule | Player hears it from | Shown by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Fire is not lantern light, but it is company.** Fire keeps you warm and can't relight a dead lamp. Lantern light, the Old Light from under the land, keeps the dark back. A hearth beside a lantern keeps it from being alone. | Hesketh, opening | His dead lamps that fire won't relight; your lamp steadying by his fire |
| 2 | **Kept light can be called. Given light cannot.** A light is *given* when it is lit for a person you could name. A light kept for yourself, or for no one, or for anyone at all (a duty to whoever passes, a lighthouse for whichever ship) is *kept*. The song calls only what it can hear, and it hears a kept light by its flame: a kept light banked down to a coal under ash, like a fire banked for the night, isn't heard, and gives no light either. Elowen's is the only one known to have lasted that way, and the Voice's song can call it out of its lamp. A lamp lit from a given flame, for someone, is given too. | Elowen, end of Ch1 | The Lanternfall night; the Order's road lamps; Maren's lamp; yours |
| 3 | **A given light goes out only when it is alone, and only by the Voice's own hands.** Shadowborn and Champions can wear a given light down until it gutters. Only the Voice can put it out, and only when its bearer has no other light in sight. A hearth, a candle or a lamp counts. People don't. The Voice can't hear a given light. It comes only where it's shown one: by a Shadowborn that has seen it in the open, or by a great light or noise. A light behind a shut door, inside a storm, or on Lantern Hill, where no seam opens, can't be shown. | Maren, without words (Ch1 a4); said plainly by Elowen | Mossy Hollow's ten years (hearths in every window); Hesketh's fire; the Long Night; Ch5; the finale |
| 4 | **The dark has no shape of its own.** What climbs out of it copies shapes it finds: the place, the things in it, and the shapes in people's memory. It copies shapes, never voices. | Anselm (Ch1 a3) | Ossuary Marshal; Emberlea's ash; Rowan's shape |
| 5 | **The dark has no voice of its own.** It speaks only with voices given to it by people who said yes to one of its offers. A yes costs your voice. The Voice lends held voices to its Elders and to some Champions. When the one holding a voice is beaten, a living person's voice goes home to them; a dead person's voice goes to rest. The first yes was said before the dark could speak, so nobody offered it; nobody knows who said it. **A voice can only go where its owner's road is open.** The first voice's owner went down the stair long ago, so when the Voice is beaten that one voice has nowhere to go and stays with what's left of the dark. | Morwen (hint, Ch1 a5); Silas (Ch2); the cost in full Ch4 a5 | The Oracle; the Fogbound; the Silent Village |
| 6 | **Held things are held, not destroyed.** On the Lanternfall night every called light flew toward the Lea; a few fell short and were caught on the way (in the marsh, in the lagoon). Freed, a light flies back to the lamp it left. Held a long time, it forgets the way, and has to be given a road. People the dark holds (Silas, Durand, Ada and Pell in the fog) are held the same way: they don't age, eat or remember the years. | Ch1 end (a few), Ch2 a5, Ch3 (thousands) | |

Rules 2 and 3 are the heart, and the player only ever needs two sentences of them: **"A light lit for someone can't be
called. A light on its own can be put out."** Rules 4 to 6 are for writers; players meet them as events.

Rule 3 answers every "why didn't the dark just…?". Your lamp held ten years because it hung among a village of hearths.
Tonight the Briar Regent shut the village into its cellars and put the hearths out one by one, so your lamp was about to
be alone; you ran with it, alone, and it guttered until Hesketh's fire. On the road it is safe while a lamp you lit
stays in sight behind you. The Voice can't hear it, so it has to be shown where you are (by the Fenmother's fall in Ch1,
Veyr's glass in Ch3), and it can only finish you alone, which is what Chapter 5 is.

### 3.3 The Lantern Order and its Oath (the hidden twist)

The Order's Oath was **"Hold the road. Keep the light. Give it to no one."** For centuries it was a vow of duty: the
Order kept every road lamp, the waymarks and the Great Lanterns, for whoever passed. No lamp was lit for anyone. **That
is why the Voice could take the whole road in one night**: every Order lamp was kept, and kept light can be called.
Elowen broke the Oath. She gave the Mother Lamp away in sparks, each lit for a stranger, and that is the only reason any
light survived. The road songs say the Saint *let the lantern fall*. The Order's survivors call her an oathbreaker. Both
are true. The player learns this in Chapter 2 (section 8.2) and sees the road differently from then on: every waymark
you relight is the Oath being undone, one lamp at a time, because you light each one for someone.

### 3.4 What the dark is, and what Shadowborn are

The dark is not night. It is a cold thickness that sits in low places and spreads at dusk. It was here before the first
lamp, and it wants the land the way it was: no light anywhere. Where a lamp burns, it cannot be. Section 5 describes the
dark's ranks: the Voice, its Elders, Champions, Captains and Shadowborn.

### 3.5 The Lanternfall night (the truth, for writers)

- For centuries the Voice gathered small yeses: travellers who sat down in the marsh fog to rest, a king's court behind a
  curtain, keepers who were tired. Each yes gave it a voice. By the Fall it had a choir.
- **Ten years ago it sang**, to put the whole land out in one night. Every kept light rose out of its lamp and flew east.
  The Order's road lamps went first. They came down on the Lea like falling stars, and the Lea has burned with them since.
- **What held:** given lights. The Barrow Lamp (Maren lit it for the dead she buried). Hesketh's lamp on the hill (for his
  wife). And the sparks of the **Mother Lamp**: in the chapel, Elowen felt it pull at her hand, broke its flame and
  pressed each spark into the hands of someone running past in the dark, lit *for* them. She gave away about forty. One
  went into the hands of the one carrying you. You were very small. The last spark she **kept for herself**, and turned
  down to a coal under ash, so the song passed over it (rule 2). Kept, it can be called the moment it burns bright; that is why she has hidden in the dark chapel for ten years.
- **After the Fall** the dark hunted the given lights the slow way. Shadowborn found each one and wore it down; when its
  bearer was alone, out of sight of any other light, the Voice's hands came. Most of Elowen's forty went out in the first
  years, carried by people who fled alone. **This winter the last one in sight of Mossy Hollow went out.** (Other given lights still burn far off, hidden: Maren's, Hesketh's wife's, Ada's candle, Nan's.) Yours hung over a door in Mossy
  Hollow, among a village of hearths, and it held. Tonight the dark came for it.

## 4. The hero

### 4.1 Who you are

You grew up in **Mossy Hollow**, a hedge village at the bottom of the Hollow, under a small lamp over your door that
never went out. You are the starter you pick, Wren, Tobin or Pip; the main story reads the same for all three, and each
hero has a line of their own at every big moment (4.6; about 25 moments, so about 75 lines). Your family is never named; the village raised you.

### 4.2 The arc

| | |
|---|---|
| **Want** (Ch1) | Keep the dark away from the people you love. You do it by carrying the lamp away from them. |
| **Flaw** | **"I carry it so nobody else has to."** You take the danger on alone and call it a gift. |
| **Need** | Let others carry light too. A light alone goes out; a light given and shared holds. |
| **Tested** | Ch1: running alone with the lamp is what nearly gets it smothered; Hesketh's fire saves it. Ch2: Hallam asks to carry the lens up the two hundred steps, and you let him (a first). Ch3: the Pyre's door opens for you both, and Caedmon asks to go first; you let him, which is harder. Ch4: the Reach lights your lamp for you, and you let them. Ch5: the flaw comes back. At the Stillwood you send Asta back up the road so she'll be safe, and Hild's candle goes with her. Your lamp now has nothing in sight, and it gutters. That is the opening the Voice needed: from here it can find you. It still wants your yes first (Coldhearth, the Offer), and only at the fight's second phase do its hands close and the lamp go out. |
| **Final answer** | The Voice: "Every flame goes out." The hero lights a taper from the returning light, in its face: "Then we light another." |

### 4.3 The hero's choices

An idle RPG with no dialogue tree still needs decisions that cost something and pay off later. Three, all cheap in data:

1. **"Lit for" at each Great Lantern** (Ch1, Ch2, Ch3). When you set your flame in a Great Lantern you choose who it is
   lit for, from the people you have met in that chapter (for example Hesketh, Tam or Bram in the Hollow; Hallam, Maren or
   Nell on the Coast). The name goes on the Great Lantern card and its Codex page. In the finale, those lamps answer
   first, by name. Different saves, different names.
2. **The Reach reverses it** (Ch4). The Reach lights its new Great Lantern *for you*. You don't choose; you receive.
3. **Coldhearth** (Ch5 a5). Your lamp is guttering, alone. You can keep all your flame, or light one cold hearth in
   Coldhearth from it (and Morwen's candle, if you carry it). Lighting it costs you: the lamp burns low for the rest of the
   chapter (a dimmer lamp icon on the fight screen, no stat change). Both branches pay off. If you lit it, the Voice's
   last offer says "You gave it away. It won't last," and in the finale the very first fire to answer is that hearth, two
   hundred years cold. If you kept it, the Voice says "You kept it all. Alone. Good," and the first to answer is Hesketh's
   rack, because he never stopped lighting it for you.

None of these change power, rewards or progress. They change words, a lamp icon and stills.

**The lamp icon** is the one place the story touches the fight screen: a small lamp by the hero's name, lit, guttering
(alone, Ch5) or out (the Voice's phase 2). One icon with three states, no animation, drawn from the approved icon pack or a vetted Codex set (art freeze); until
then a text tag.

**Rules for writers.**
- **Zone lines** say why *this* monster is *here*: the shape it copied and the light it is after. They name the monster.
- **Captain lines** name the one harm this Captain does ("It holds the seam open"; "It swallowed the well lamp").
  The area's **fifth Captain** always points at the Champion ("The rest answer to the throne in the green").
- **Champion scenes** say what it does for its Elder; its **pre** promises the area's turn (what you'll get back); its
  **post** shows the light it was holding come back, and one line of the Elder answering. That line makes the Elder a
  felt presence for the whole chapter, and names nothing the player hasn't seen. Each chapter has one motif:
  Ch1 "Far off, the air turns colder." Ch2 "Out at sea, the fog bank creeps closer." Ch3 "On the horizon, the red glow
  brightens." Ch4 "The wind changes. Somewhere, a door slams." Ch5 "Behind you, another waymark dims."
- **Who answers to whom** is a Journal page that fills in a row the first time the player meets each rank, so the chain
  is visible without a card.
- **Elders** are not Shadowborn (except the Whitehush, which is the storm's own walker). Each is the region's argument.
- **Bosses never repeat** (owner decision). The Voice's ranks are beaten once, in story order.

### 5.1 The Voice

The dark that was here first, and the one part of it that learned to speak, with voices people gave it. For a thousand
years it has gathered the voices of everyone who said yes, and that choir is what sang the song. It does not hate people;
it hardly sees them. It wants the land still and dark and quiet. Its one belief is plain and partly right: **every flame
goes out.**

**Its five offers.** Each is a way to be alone with your light, and each sells a different loneliness. The hero hears
each one once and answers with their own line.

| Ch | Offer | The loneliness it sells | Who said yes, and the price the player sees |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"Rest."** | Exhaustion: "You're tired. Nobody is coming." | Travellers who sat down in the marsh fog. Their voices fill the Fenmother. The dead ones never come back. |
| 2 | **"Forever."** | Duty: "You're the only keeper. Only you can hold it." | Silas Penrow, who kept the Saltreach light alone and wrote letters he never sent. It never went out. It burned green and drowned ships. |
| 3 | **"Safe."** | Fear of loss: "Don't share it. Sharing is how it's stolen." | Ser Durand, who wanted Emberlea's lights safe from the Wyrm. They were so safe no one could have them, and the Lea burned for ten years. |
| 4 | **"Yours."** | Fear of others: "Shut the door. They'd take it." | The Silent Village, one family at a time, in the storm. Their candles were called, then their voices taken. |
| 5 | **"Let go."** | Despair: "Everyone alone ends the same. Come to the end now." | Offered to you. Coldhearth shows the end it means. |

## 9. The ending of Season 1

### 9.1 Victory

The Voice's last hands open. The light pours in, and the choir leaves it, thousands of voices at once: the living ones home
to their owners, the dead to rest. The lullaby goes to rest; for a moment you know the tune, then you don't. What's left is
small. It speaks once more, in a voice no one has heard in this story: not a monster's, not a choir's. A small, tired,
ordinary voice, like a person's. **"Every flame goes out. I can wait."** The hero lights a taper from the returning light
and holds it up in its face (a still): **"Then we light another."** (per hero, 4.6). That voice has no home to go to. It
sinks into the ground at the valley's heart, and goes down.

### 9.2 The warm beat

One line per card, a tap each, all skippable, never replayed. Text on a plain card with the lamp icon; two stills (the empty hook, Maud's Lantern).
1. The Seam opens wide. The sky over the Gloamvale is ordinary night. Real stars.
2. Across the land, at dawn, people who haven't spoken in years wake up and speak. Doors open in villages you never saw.
3. Anselm rings Patience: the last toll. "That's the one I kept."
4. Elowen leaves her spark turned up. Hesketh doesn't like it. She: "It's going where the light is. Let it think I'm the one
   guarding it."
5. Vesper finishes the song: "And the dark went down, and the lamps came on. / There. That's the ending. For now."
6. You walk back into Mossy Hollow. The hook over your door has been empty since the night you left (a still). You light a
   new lamp from yours and hang it there. Tam is waiting. "This one's lit for you, Tam." (Lit for a named person, so it can't be called.)
7. At the gate fire, Asta has finished another cairn.
8. Hesketh at the fire: "Every road needs a place to come back to."
9. After the credits card: at the bottom of the Deepwell, Maud's Lantern flickers for the first time (a still).

### 9.3 Why this satisfies

- Both questions are answered: the light was lit for you, and it was for giving.
- The hero's arc closes: the one who carried it alone, and chose to again at the Stillwood, is saved by every lamp they lit
  or hung for someone, starting with the one the player chose at Coldhearth.
- Every recurring character pays off in the finale (Hesketh's rack and note, Elowen's spark, Anselm's toll, Vesper's verse,
  Thessaly's vision, Corvin with Ada's lamp, Tam at the door).
- The Voice loses the fight plainly. Its claim is partly true and stays partly true: **Rowan, Thessaly's mother, Ashby's
  husband, Cass's brother and Saltreach's drowned stay lost.** The answer is not "flames don't go out", it's "light another".
- Home, which the hero ran from on the first night, is lit again by their hand.

### 9.4 What stays true after

Night still comes, and in the story Shadowborn still climb out of old seams (how areas replay is a game rule, not a story
one). Everything won stays won. The Voice is gone from the Gloamvale. It is not gone.

  The season needs **seven stills**, one Codex pack: (1) the lamp on its hook with the spiral (opening), (2) the empty hook
  (homecoming), (3) the lamp going out ("Nothing."), (4) the road of lamps answering, (5) the Reach's Great Lantern lit for
  you, (6) the taper held up to the Voice, (7) Maud's Lantern flickering. **Everything else** (the Kelp rescue, Liss's
  frost, Coldhearth, the warm beat, the Champion and NPC scenes) is text on a plain card with the area background and the
  lamp icon. **The Lamp Rack** is a row of existing lamp icons in the camp panel, one per area cleared; a drawn rack is an
  art request for the owner, not a dependency. Until the pack is vetted, every card is text-only and the story still works.
- **10.4 Switch-off.** One flag turns the whole story layer off: `STORY_ON` in `55-story.js` (dev), and a player option
  "Story cards: on / off" in Settings. Off, no card plays, the Journal keeps what was read, and the `story` keys are kept
  but ignored, so turning it back on loses nothing. Rollback is the same flag in a one-line PR; the story data files
  (`21k-story-*.js`) can also be removed without touching any other system. The `story-delivery` card builds both.
- **10.4b Data and save.** C28 7.1 (`STORY_BEATS`), one data file per chapter (`21k-story-hollow.js`, `21k-story-coast.js`
  and so on), plus `voice`, `hero` (keyed `wren`, `tobin`, `pip`), `choice`, `letters`, `pages`, `npc` and `notes` entries.
  New keys under the existing `story` state, merged with defaults, never a repurposed field: `starter` (set once at the
  cold start; existing saves take the first starter they own), `litFor` (one name per Great Lantern), `coldhearth` (lit or
  kept), `ends` (sequence id to `done` or `skipped`, 12a) and `journalOpens` (a count, 12a). The Lamp Rack is derived from
  progress, not stored. Needs an Opus save review. Hero-unlock gates keep heroes an
  existing save owns.
- **10.5 Only what's in the game.** A zone line needs its roster monster in `ZONE_FOES`; Champion and Elder scenes need their
  encounter. Until then the slot is silent. **Prerequisite:** the code still runs the old 7-place cycle (zone 8 is "Mossy
  Hollow II"); zone and area *names* must move to the 7-areas-of-5 structure before area titles can play (audit, "The
  structural blocker"). Backgrounds don't move: the owner decided zones 1-7 use the Mossy Hollow scenery, and the art
  freeze holds the rest. So no story line describes scenery; lines name the place and say what happened there.
- **10.6 Volume and order.** About 35 area titles, 175 zone lines, 175 Captain lines, 70 Champion cards, 5 Elder sequences,
  4 Great Lantern sequences, about 40 NPC scenes, 35 Journal pages, about 75 hero lines, 10 Voice lines and the finale. Story
  ships region by region, behind the region's content: Chapter 1 for Milestone 1, then each chapter's script lands when
  its region is built.

## 11. Season 2: room to continue

### 11.1 What Season 1 closes

The land's lights are home. The Elders are beaten. The choir is broken up. The hero knows what their lamp is and what it is
for. The Oath is undone, one waymark at a time.

### 11.2 What Season 1 opens (new questions, not unfinished ones)

1. **Whose was that small, tired voice?** Someone said yes first, before anyone could have offered it, and gave the dark
   its first voice. Who? Planted by rule 5 (Ch2 Elder), Corvin's "a voice behind a curtain" (Ch5 a4) and the voice itself.
2. **Where did it go?** Into the Deepwell, under Hollow's Rest. The last still, after the credits card: at the bottom of the
   Deepwell, **Maud's Lantern flickers for the first time.** Planted by Grenna's "further than our ropes", the Deep Lore line
   "The stair goes further than any rope has measured", and Morwen's family name.
3. **Elowen is bait.** Her spark is the one she kept, so it can be called, and she has left it turned up so the Voice will
   come to her. What is she planning, and is she right?
4. **The lullaby.** It went to rest, so its singer is dead, and they once said yes. Who sang it to you, and what did they
   say yes to? (The one who carried you stays unnamed; Season 2 may never name them either.)
5. **The Order's survivors** want the Oath back. Aldric is torn.
6. **The dark's ranks are broken, not gone.** Shadowborn still climb out of old seams and the Captains are only
   driven down. The few Champions whose post scenes show them sinking rather than breaking (the Chained Star, Mora,
   Sevrin) are named in the Journal as "went down" and are Season 2's first faces. Bosses never repeat in Season 1.

Long-play players quit over an empty endgame (research, `fun-library.md` section 8), so these are concrete threads
with names on them, not a fog of "more to come". Recurring people each end Season 1 with one open line: Elowen (bait),
Aldric (the Oath), Grenna (the well cover), Morwen (Maud), Kestrel (she means to walk the high pass the storm took), Oriel ("one star is
moving under the ground").

### 11.3 Lore that unlocks in pieces

Each Champion beaten files one Journal page; each area cleared files its zone and Captain lines in the Bestiary; each
Elder files a verse of Vesper's song. A chapter's pages read as one story when complete, and the Journal shows the
gaps ("2 pages missing in the Hollow"). Locked things name what opens them in story terms ("Opens when the Hollow's
Elder falls"), sharing the hint pattern of the `ap-first-use-hints` card.

### 11.4 Rules Season 2 inherits

The six rules stay the rules. No resets. Tone stays warm. The Voice is never destroyed off screen.

## 12a. Predicted effect, and how it's measured

The design-doc rubric asks for a number. Chapter 1 is the first test, once `story-opening`, `story-delivery` and
`story-hollow-script` are in and the browser playtest bots (`f-playtest-bots`) run.
- **Comprehension:** after zone 5 on a fresh save, at least 4 of 5 new-player persona runs can answer "Why are you
  fighting?" with the lamp and the dark coming for it. Measure: the bots' end-of-run questions in their playtest report.
  Missed: 2 or fewer.
- **Interest:** the story Skip rate on Champion scenes in Chapter 1 stays under 40% across persona runs, and at least one
  Journal page is opened by zone 35. Missed: Skip over 60%, or no Journal opens.
  Measure: `seen`/`read` can't, because a card is marked read the moment it opens (`openBeat()` in `75-story-ui.js`).
  So `story-delivery` adds two save counters (10.4b): `story.ends`, one entry per multi-card sequence (Champion, Elder,
  Great Lantern, finale), set to `done` when its last card is closed and `skipped` when Skip or Close ends it early; and
  `story.journalOpens`. It also adds the reader, `tools/story-stats.mjs`, which takes a save file or save code and prints
  the Chapter 1 Champion skip rate and Journal opens. Sources: the playtest bots' end-of-run saves (their personas choose
  when to skip, so these test the plumbing), then the real number from Cal's and the testers' saves, sent as save codes.
  `tools/health.mjs` (sim personas, no story UI) does not measure this.
- **Cal's play prompt:** after Milestone 1, Cal's one-question prompt ("Did the Fenmother feel like the end of a
  chapter?") gets a yes. A no starts an Opus diagnosis thread (playbook).

## 13. How the story keeps being built and reviewed

### 13.1 The depth bar

[`docs/review/story.md`](../review/story.md) scores story work on twelve criteria. The bible and every chapter script must
reach **4 or more on every criterion and an average of 4.3 or more** from the Opus story judge. Everyday story copy uses the
`content` rubric plus the story rubric's hard checks.

### 13.2 The loop

1. **Write** (Sonnet 5.5 medium): a chapter script or story change, from this bible.
2. **Red team** (Sonnet 5.5 medium, argues against).
3. **Judge** (Opus 5.5 high, read-only): scores against `story.md`, rules on the red team, lists what a pass needs.
4. **Revise**, and repeat 2 and 3 until it passes, at most four rounds; after that the judge decides what ships and the rest
   becomes a follow-up card.
5. **Canon:** anything that changes this bible is a `judge` gate change, recorded in the PR and in `docs/DECISIONS.md` as
   "Claude decided", and reported in the digest for Cal's veto.

### 13.3 Standing checks

- **Weekly design review** (Opus high; `autopilot/playbook.md`): a story coherence check every week: the week's merged text
  against this bible and the ladder (12), the story check's output, and one finding about the weakest stretch of story still
  in the game.
- **`tools/check.mjs` "story"** (built by the delivery card): limits, required fields, every zone line names its monster, no
  retired words, no beat keyed to a companion, no text for content that isn't in the game, hero lines for all three starters,
  every choice has a default.
