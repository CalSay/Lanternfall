# C22 Coast: darkness-born encounter candidates

Concept exploration for owner and Claude review, 1 October 2026. **This is not a final roster or count.** Every candidate is one visible enemy in one active fight; there are no gauntlets, queued reinforcements, Auto battles or offline combat rewards. The owner has superseded the earlier “corrupted wildlife and drowned villagers” direction. Ordinary Coast foes here emerge as distinct beings from the Shroud and the sea's altered light. “Shroud” names the established darkness and its manifestation, not a new regional boss, hidden mastermind or extra encounter. Hunting alone retains corrupted wildlife. Silas Penrow is the deliberate story exception: his established human history and three-phase boss proposal stay intact in [the earlier Coast draft](enemies-c22-coast.md).

The labels A and B below mean provisional first-look and reserve concepts for each of the seven areas, **not** a commitment to one or two foes per area. Their mechanics are design targets from the shared [C22 contract](enemies-c22-contract.md): ordinary toughness about 3–5 neutral uncritical Attack actions, normal missed-move damage about 20–35% of a zone-ready reference hero's HP, fixed zone values at implementation, one actor, every real hit parryable or dodgeable, and a legible tell per hit. A named tougher variant, if selected later, inherits its parent's body and two moves, raises toughness to about 6–8 actions, and adds one distinctly cued move; it is never a second actor or a mandatory parallel slot. These names and damage numbers remain proposals for step-3 calibration.

**Combat shorthand for this brief.** Physical armour 0/1/2 proposes 0%/10%/20% reduction to physical damage only; opening a named seam changes 2 to 1, never grants immunity. All listed hit percentages are per-hit shares of the zone-ready reference hero's maximum HP before defence and mitigation, converted to fixed zone stats in step 3. Bleed 1 ticks 1% reference HP at the start of each of the next two hero opportunities; Venom 1 ticks 1% for the next three; Burn 1 ticks 2% for the next two. Chill 1 lowers Speed 10% for two hero opportunities, maximum two stacks; Weaken 1 lowers direct output 25% for the next hero opportunity; Blind 1 gives the next direct hero action a 30% miss chance without hiding a cue or affecting parry/dodge. A rider applies only when its named hit lands, and reapplication refreshes the strongest effect rather than stacking hidden damage. These provisional magnitudes are not final C19 status balance.

All ordinary candidates award Gold and Essence, with only existing eligible relic rolls. No wood, fibre, herbs, gems, ore, hide, Tide Sigil, or other gathered material comes from a kill. Elders retain their existing Trophies, approved Gold/Essence/relic eligibility, and occasional existing uniques; no new drop rate is proposed. Existing first-clear story flags remain. Each normal needs at most seven body keys (idle, two wind-ups, two contacts, hurt, defeat), plus independently readable hit FX; a retained elder may reuse the body language but needs a unique charge hold and release. No art or implementation is authorized by this exploration.

## Grey Shingle — armour and the tide line

**A · Breakwater Myrmidon.** A salt-plate warrior hatches fully armed from a black seam between wave-cut stones; it has a narrow glowing sternum, four hinged forearms and no human face or animal shell. Speed 0.75×, toughness 5, physical armour 2 while folded/1 while open, weak frost. **Plate Shear:** two physical contacts, 12% + 14% (26 total); left and right forearm plates rise one after the other, exposing the sternum only after the second. **Breaker Ram:** one delayed 31% physical hit; all four arms clamp to the chest, then the exposed shoulder drops before the lunge. It teaches attacking the opening and defending a single heavy beat. Wren Mark, Tobin's two-hit parry, and Pip's Frost Shard each answer a different part. Art must separate the four arms from the stone background; it is not a crab in armour.

**B · Tideglass Knave.** A thin bipedal shard-creature grows from a standing sheet of dark tide, with two sickle fins where hands should be and a visible hollow core. Speed 1.35×, toughness 3, armour 0, weak poison. **Fin Cut:** one quick 22% physical hit; its right blade flashes upright before contact. **Crosswake:** two hits, 11% + 13% frost (24 total); the left fin first scores a line in the water and the right fin reaches across after a held beat. Its speed tests two separate reaction choices, never an invisible splash hit. A single dry rock is its contact point, so the hero can reach it; its defeat collapses into unlootable seawater and light.

## Gullcliffs — height and storm pressure

**A · Tempest Roclet.** A juvenile storm-roc condensed from thundercloud and black salt, with six hooked lightning talons and a solid beak-shaped storm core; it is a fantasy creature, not a changed gull. Speed 1.35×, toughness 4, armour 0, weak poison. **Talon Crossing:** two physical hits, 12% + 13% (25 total); two distinct talon shadows land across the same shelf. **Squall Fold:** one delayed 29% frost hit; its cloud-wings fold into a bright vertical blade, then open at the contact cue. The player reads the returning perch, not a flying offscreen attacker. Wren Pinning Shot can slow its next gauge fill, Tobin parries the crossing, and Pip attacks while it is perched. Art needs a storm-core silhouette and a grounded return pose.

**B · Gale Cyclops.** A long-legged wind elemental with one huge shuttering eye and stone-hook feet; the cliffs' pressure gives it a body, with no buried human or ordinary bird underneath. Speed 1.0×, toughness 5, armour 1, weak holy. **Skyhammer:** one delayed 31% physical hit; its eye closes, both hook feet plant, and a compact arm of wind descends. **Iris Flash:** two frost hits, 11% + 14% (25 total); the eye opens by two visible segments, with Blind 1 only on the second landed hit. Its large eye must be readable at sprite scale without relying on colour. Wren can punish the planted pause, Tobin handles the two beats, and Pip's direct fire remains neutral.

## The Wrecks — abyssal intruders, not drowned crew

**A · Keelwrought Reaver.** A black-armoured raider emerges from the Shroud inside a wreck's shadow; ribs are open seawater, while its hooked arm and trailing cord are living limbs rather than salvaged tools or a possessed corpse. Speed 0.85×, toughness 4, armour 1, weak holy. **Hook Chop:** one slow 27% physical hit; the hook lifts above its blank helm. **Cord Snap:** two hits, 12% + 14% physical (26 total); the cord first pulls taut, then lashes across the hull, with Bleed 1 only on the second landed hit. The enemy tests a held second strike. Wren can Mark its exposed ribs, Tobin may parry both, and Pip can use direct fire. Its cord and hook must never look like a third offscreen actor.

**B · Bilge Gremlin.** One three-armed, barrel-chested abyssal imp squeezes out of a seam of black bilge; its teeth are lantern-glass triangles and its tail is a living rudder. Speed 1.4×, toughness 3, armour 0, weak poison. **Glass Bite:** one fast 23% physical hit, cued by the triangular mouth opening. **Boarding Scurry:** three physical hits, 8% + 8% + 9% (25 total); each arm plants in order on the deck before a separate swipe. This is one body and one move, not a rat swarm. Wren can Pin the fast approach, Tobin earns individual parry refunds, and Pip can end its short fight with Spark. Art needs three distinct arm-contact silhouettes.

## Kelp Shallows — living abyssal forms at the waterline

**A · Tangle Hydra.** A dark sea-born hydra rises where the kelp's shadow knots: one central body, three frond-necked heads, and a clearly marked real jaw. It is an emergent monster, not an eel bound in weeds. Speed 1.0×, toughness 4, armour 0, weak poison/resist frost. **Frond Lash:** two physical hits, 12% + 14% (26 total); two outer heads part, and only the second landed hit gives Weaken 1. **Core Bite:** one delayed 28% physical hit; the central jaw emerges after the false outer-head motion, with a real contact cue at the jaw. The test is finding the attack source without creating three targetable foes. Wren Marks the core, Tobin Braces a selected hit, and Pip's direct fire stays neutral.

**B · Reef Harpooner.** A chitin-limbed abyssal lancer grows from the dark beneath a tide rock, carrying a spear-shaped forearm that cannot be dropped or looted. Its shoulders flare like a reef crown, unlike any drowned diver. Speed 0.95×, toughness 5, armour 1, weak holy. **Barb Thrust:** one delayed 30% physical hit; both split hooves plant on dry stone before the forearm extends. **Cold Line:** two frost hits, 13% + 15% (28 total); a visible webbed tendon straightens twice, applying Chill 1 on the final landed hit but never dragging or skipping the hero. Wren can pierce the shoulder seam, Tobin reads the planted thrust, and Pip can attack through frost neutrality. Show tendon and forearm separately in art.

## Glimmer Lagoon — stolen light given new bodies

**A · Mirror Siren.** A faceless, fin-crowned being condenses out of green reflections; the lagoon's darkness forms a single translucent torso around a hard black heart. It is neither jellyfish nor dead bather. Speed 0.85×, toughness 4, armour 0, weak holy/resist poison. **Heart Sting:** one quick 24% poison hit; the black heart compresses and a single pointed ray leaves it, with Venom 1 only if it lands. **Glass Pulse:** two frost hits, 12% + 14% (26 total); two rings travel from its crown after separate contractions, with Blind 1 on the second landed hit. The player sees the actual body even when reflections multiply harmlessly. Wren Power Shot, Tobin's double parry, and Pip's fire-neutral damage all work.

**B · Pearl Golem.** A hulking creature of black nacre, chalk and inner blue light extrudes itself from a lagoon fault; it is no oyster enlarged by corruption. Two massive halves form shoulders around a visible central hinge. Speed 0.7×, toughness 5, physical armour 2 closed/1 open, weak frost/resist poison. **Hinge Crush:** one delayed 32% physical hit; shoulders open until the hinge turns white, then clap on a single cue. **Nacre Hail:** two physical hits, 13% + 14% (27 total); separate bright nodules rise from each shoulder before flight, with Weaken 1 on the second landed hit. Wren Marks the hinge, Tobin can parry Crush, and Pip exploits frost. The creature drops no Gems despite its body.

## Drowned Saltreach — a town besieged by things from the water

**A · Brine Oracle.** A six-fingered dark-sea seer walks out of a vertical sheet of saltwater wearing a face of faceted green glass. It was never a village witch; its ritual copies human prayer imperfectly. Speed 0.9×, toughness 4, armour 0, weak holy/resist frost. **Salt Hex:** one delayed 22% poison hit; all six fingers close around a single brine ring, adding Weaken 1 only on contact. **Glass Lash:** two frost hits, 12% + 14% (26 total); two wrist rotations give distinct contact cues, with Chill 1 only on the second landed hit. Wren can pre-Mark for Sonic Arrow, Tobin reads both wrists, and Pip's direct fire stays neutral. Art requires an inhuman six-finger silhouette, not a woman with a colour swap.

**B · Saltglass Harrier.** A wingless imp of salt needles and black membrane springs from a drowned doorway; its paired mandibles and four angular feet make it unmistakably a Shroud creature, not a mite. Speed 1.4×, toughness 3, armour 0, weak poison. **Twin Nip:** two quick poison hits, 11% + 12% (23 total); the mandibles spread left and right in sequence, Venom 1 on the second landed hit. **Lintel Leap:** one fast-delayed 23% physical hit; four feet scrape twice on stone before the actual spring. The apparent early scrape must not consume a valid reaction window. Wren's Twin Shot can cut it down, Tobin's Brace covers the leap, and Pip can Spark between lunges.

## Coral Nave — the Shroud's court before Silas

**A · Reef Paladin.** The Shroud grows an armoured knight from coral, salt and empty blue flame to guard the ruined pews; there is no human guardian inside. Two heavy palms and a luminous joint make its combat silhouette distinct from the Breakwater Myrmidon. Speed 0.75×, toughness 5, physical armour 2 folded/1 exposed, weak frost/resist poison. **Stone Palm:** one delayed 32% physical hit; the joint glows before the downward strike. **Spire Fan:** three physical hits, 9% + 10% + 11% (30 total); three individually cued coral spires lift from its own forearm, Bleed 1 on the last landed hit. This tests a heavy single versus three smaller defences. Wren Mark, Tobin's all-parry counter, and Pip's frost each have purpose. Keep every spire attached to the one actor.

**B · Choral Seraph.** A six-throated, wingless spirit unfolds from a fault in the nave's shadow; its voices are its own, not echoes of drowned worshippers. Speed 1.1×, toughness 4, armour 0, weak holy/resist frost. **Broken Chord:** two frost hits, 12% + 14% (26 total); two of six throats open in order and the second landed note adds Blind 1. **Lament Spear:** one delayed 26% poison hit; all throats close, a single pointed sound-wave condenses at the chest and only then fires, adding Weaken 1 if it lands. Wren can shorten the song with Echo Shot, Tobin parries two notes, and Pip's Fireball remains neutral. Its six mouths need distinct but economical poses; no invisible choir actors.

## Elder directions and Silas

These are **candidate replacements**, not seven approved required bosses. Each retains one visible body, an explicit charge opportunity with at least one hero response, separate defence on every release hit, and the C22 default interrupt of non-locked control or 6% actual boss HP after charge start. Interruption spends the next opportunity on recovery, with an ordinary move before another charge. The named existing Trophy and occasional unique stay available only under their approved rules.

This table is not a final moveset. Silas alone retains his story, lens, Green Beam, Drowned Bell, three phases and Great Lantern unlock. He awards no gathering material and never calls another enemy on stage; the prior Bosun deckhand queue and every gauntlet suggestion are superseded.

None of the earlier concepts survives with its old origin unchanged: fish, crabs, gulls, rats and other ordinary wildlife belong to Hunting if corrupted; drowned townspeople retain their tragedy in lore and scenery, not as the new routine enemy source. Even the old wisps/echoes were described as dead people or trapped sailor light, so their origins need changing.
