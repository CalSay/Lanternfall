# Hollow: darkness-born enemy concept review

Exploration draft, 1 October 2026. **Final roster count and selection are pending the owner's discussion with Claude.** These fourteen pitches compare different possibilities; they are not fourteen approved enemies, an allocation of two enemies per area, or a production commitment. The older Hollow roster is superseded as a selection proposal. No art or runtime is changed.

## Direction and constraints

Combat enemies emerged with the darkness. They are its hunters, soldiers and sorcerous creations. They do not become ordinary wildlife or rescued villagers when defeated: their unnatural bodies unravel and the stolen light escapes. Hunting retains existing animals driven into a rage by the dark and stays separate from combat. Fenmother retains her established story exception pending coordinator review.

One enemy per fight. No gauntlets, queued replacements, adds, automatic next fights or Hunting combat cards. Repeated duels against a strong identity are welcome. The first chosen Mossy Hollow enemy takes about three ordinary uncritical hits. Gold, Essence and existing relic eligibility remain; bosses may occasionally drop uniques and elders keep existing Trophies. No material drops.

Every candidate has a specific physical attack, a contrasting second move and a silhouette that explains both. Every real contact permits parry or dodge. Feints cause no damage. One successful parry refunds one turn from every cooldown; a counter requires the whole move to be parried. No new status, extra reaction button, positioning system or procedural limb animation is assumed.

## Candidate cards, grouped by place

Numbers are provisional calibration against a zone-ready hero, not measured balance or damage that scales with the player's HP. Toughness counts ordinary uncritical attacks; Speed is relative to a hero at 1.0. Physical armour is a percentage reduction. Normal caps remain two consecutive opportunities. Alternate the two named moves unless a card says otherwise. Status riders require the named hit to land; repeating one refreshes its duration rather than stacking hidden damage. Venom/Bleed ticks below are 2% reference HP at each of two hero starts. Weaken is 25% reduced direct output for one hero opportunity. Unlisted damage types are neutral.

### Mossy Hollow: Thorn Imp

**Pitch:** a small, malicious emissary with a split wooden mask, hooked horn and two oversized thorn blades growing from its forearms. A dark seam joins its floating chest plates; it has never been a tree, child or animal. Its crouched silhouette makes a memorable first opponent without starting with an enormous boss.

**Combat:** three hits to defeat; Speed 0.9; armour 0%; weak fire, resists poison. **Briar Jab:** one 20% physical hit; it pulls one blade beside its mask, then visibly thrusts. **Crosscut:** two 10% physical hits (20% total), slow then fast; crossed forearms open one at a time. No opening status tax. Wren's Echo Shot, Tobin's Shield Bash and Pip's Fireball all work immediately; ordinary Attack and either defence suffice.

**Art:** seven keys: idle, jab wind/contact, crosscut wind/contact, hurt, unravel. Two forearm silhouettes and fixed contact planes; no stretching vine or random thorn simulation.

### Mossy Hollow: Gloomjaw

**Pitch:** a squat, two-legged maw with three interlocking petal jaws around a hollow violet throat. The darkness grows these mouths to swallow lanternlight. Its body is the jaw assembly, not an ordinary creature wearing a plant crown.

**Combat:** three hits; Speed 0.8; armour 5%; weak fire, resists poison. **Snap Shut:** one delayed 22% physical hit; three jaw plates lock outward before closing. **Spit the Light:** one 20% holy projectile hit; a stolen spark becomes a visible bead in the throat before release. Both are defendable; the second tests ranged timing without a status. Starter skills remain sufficient; Pip's fire is favoured, not mandatory.

**Art:** seven keys with distinct open-jaw and throat-bead holds. One authored spark projectile. No swallowing the hero or changing camera scale.

### Batwing Caves: Riftwing

**Pitch:** a horned, eyeless fiend whose two crescent wings form a broken ring around its exposed chest. It crawls on wing knuckles between dives; its wings fold into cutting blades rather than bird feathers.

**Combat:** four hits; Speed 1.15; armour 0%; weak holy, resists frost. **Crescent Dive:** two physical hits, 12% then 14% (26%), slow-fast; left and right wing edges flash separately. **False Descent:** a harmless lift followed by one delayed 26% physical plunge. Pinned offers a slower read; Tobin's parries reward learning both wings; Pip uses neutral fire rather than resisted frost.

**Art:** seven keys plus a single authored wind arc. Every dive returns to its reachable perch; no enemy hovering beyond melee reach.

### Batwing Caves: Maw Cantor

**Pitch:** a long-limbed cave demon with a vertical mouth splitting its entire torso. Three bright teeth hang at different heights inside it. It sings through its chest to lead travellers away from their lamps.

**Combat:** four hits; Speed 0.9; armour 0%; weak holy, resists poison. **Rib Spear:** one 28% physical stab, visibly drawing a rib into the forearm first. **Hollow Hymn:** three 8% holy pulses (24%), slow-slow-fast; the three teeth ignite in order. No silence, stolen inputs or ambient sound damage. Wren can prepare Mark; Tobin can earn three refunds; Pip's Ward cushions a missed pulse.

**Art:** seven body keys and three pulse effects; the torso remains one sprite, not a deforming mouth mesh. Reduced motion retains the numbered tooth flashes.

### The Bonefield: Ossuary Knight

**Pitch:** a soldier the darkness assembled from pale horn-like plates, with a floating skull mask and a lance that folds into its spine. It imitates a skeleton but contains no person's remains or trapped soul.

**Combat:** five hits; Speed 0.8; armour 20%; weak holy, resists poison. **Spine Lance:** one delayed 32% physical stab; the lance clicks straight before its forward contact. **Grave Salute:** two 14% physical cuts (28%), even rhythm; the mask bows then the weapon changes sides. Wren's Mark and later piercing attacks help; Tobin can Sunder after unlock; Pip bypasses physical armour with spells. Damage and ordinary defence remain viable without those upgrades.

**Art:** seven keys and one separate lance asset with folded/extended forms; no individual bone physics.

### The Bonefield: Pall Reaper

**Pitch:** a hunched fiend carried on two scythe arms, its tapering shroud concealing an empty torso. Its backward-facing crown swivels before a strike, giving the player a strange but repeatable tell.

**Combat:** four hits; Speed 1.05; armour 0%; weak holy, resists frost. **Reaping Arc:** one 27% physical hit; crown rotation ends before the arm sweeps. **Sickle Return:** two 11% physical contacts (22%), quick-delayed; the second adds Bleed, for 26% including both ticks. Wren can slow it; Tobin can guard a missed return; Pip's fire remains neutral.

**Art:** seven keys; two rigid scythe poses and a fixed shadow under the body. The shroud is authored, not simulated cloth.

### Beetle Barrows: Cryptmaw

**Pitch:** a low siege creature with overlapping tomb-shaped shields around a sideways mouth. Four hooked legs carry a vault of black flesh. It burrowed out of the darkness; it is neither a beetle nor a haunted grave door.

**Combat:** five hits; Speed 0.75; armour 20%; weak fire, resists poison. **Vault Crush:** one held 33% physical slam; the entire front shield rises and exposes the mouth. **Lockbite:** two 13% physical bites (26%), slow-fast; upper and lower jaws close separately. Wren's Mark, Tobin's Sunder or Pip's fire reward preparation. The exposed mouth is a visual cue only, not an unimplemented damage multiplier.

**Art:** seven keys; a raised front shell creates the opening without animating every shield independently. No burrowing out of the arena.

### Beetle Barrows: Shroudweaver

**Pitch:** a six-limbed, masked stalker with two weaving arms above four stabbing legs. Its cloak is a fan of taut shadow threads attached to its own spine, not a corrupted spider or a summoned swarm.

**Combat:** four hits; Speed 1.1; armour 5%; weak fire, resists frost. **Needlewalk:** three 9% physical contacts (27%), slow-fast-fast; three legs lift in a fixed sequence. **Pall Thread:** one 23% poison bolt, followed by Venom if it lands (27% including ticks); upper arms draw one visible thread before release. Parry/dodge denies the rider; a cleanse is optional. Wren's Pinned, Tobin's multi-hit refunds and Pip's fire offer different answers.

**Art:** eight keys: idle, two needle contact keys and one wind-up, thread wind/release, hurt, unravel. Static cloak fan, one thread bolt; no webbing across the floor.

### Fungal Deep: Mycelial Oracle

**Pitch:** a levitating, eyeless spellcaster whose crown resembles three enormous fungal gills. The darkness extruded it beneath the forest; it carries a ring of teeth where a face should be. Each gill opens to announce a pulse.

**Combat:** four hits; Speed 0.9; armour 0%; weak fire, resists poison. **Spore Edict:** two 11% poison bolts (22%), slow-delayed; the second adds Venom (26% including ticks). **Crown Sever:** one 29% physical slice; a rigid gill blade folds across the torso before release. Wren can interrupt once control unlocks, Tobin can guard, Pip's Fireball exploits the weakness. No permanent spore field or unavoidable room damage.

**Art:** seven keys and two spore projectiles. The spellcaster stays at melee contact height; crown states are hand-authored.

### Fungal Deep: Rot Herald

**Pitch:** a broad demon with a split antler crest and a hollow chest filled with luminous spores. Its hammer-shaped fists are part of its body; its pale roots hang free rather than attaching to a tree or animal.

**Combat:** five hits; Speed 0.8; armour 10%; weak fire, resists poison. **Hollow Hammer:** one 32% physical impact; a fist settles overhead before the downstroke. **Unsealing:** two 12% poison pulses (24%), even rhythm; both chest doors open in sequence, second hit applies one-opportunity Weaken. Wren can bank setup through the weakened action; Tobin defends the slow hammer; Pip uses fire. Chest opening does not create a new target or exposure system.

**Art:** seven keys; chest-door opening is two fixed silhouettes, not a fully rigged rib cage.

### Quarry Ruins: Riftforged Colossus

**Pitch:** a squat construct dragged through a crack in the quarry wall, its floating stone plates orbiting a chained black star. One massive piledriver arm and one small anchoring claw make its silhouette asymmetric and readable.

**Combat:** five hits; Speed 0.75; armour 25%; weak frost, resists poison. **Star Hammer:** one delayed 34% physical slam; the black star contracts, then the piledriver descends. **Shackle Volley:** three 9% physical fragments (27%), slow-slow-fast; three chain links flash before each shot. Wren's pierce or Tobin's Sunder help later; Pip's frost is favoured. No required ability, arena destruction or separate targetable core.

**Art:** eight keys including separate volley release; plate orbits are fixed offset loops and contacts authored. Its normal version fits the stage, with height saved for an elder treatment.

### Quarry Ruins: Seamstalker

**Pitch:** a narrow, four-armed fiend that looks like a black crack until it turns sideways. Its blade arms unfold from nested crystal sleeves. A bright face slit makes its facing legible even against dark stone.

**Combat:** four hits; Speed 1.15; armour 5%; weak frost, resists fire. **Fault Cut:** two 13% physical hits (26%), quick-delayed; upper arms open first, lower arms make the real second cut. **Seam Thrust:** one 29% physical hit after a harmless sideways feint. Wren can Pinned, Tobin gains two parry refunds, Pip uses Frost Shard. It never teleports behind the UI or hides the defence cue.

**Art:** eight keys for frontal/sideways hold and distinct cuts. The crack silhouette is a pose, not invisibility or procedural portal geometry.

### Wraithmarsh: Lantern Eater

**Pitch:** a tall, backward-kneed fiend with a cage of long fingers around a stolen light in its abdomen. Its head is a small crescent above the cage, giving it a silhouette unlike a ghost or drowned villager.

**Combat:** four hits; Speed 1.0; armour 0%; weak holy, resists frost. **Cage Rake:** two 13% physical hits (26%), slow-fast; fingers spread in two clear groups. **Gutter Breath:** one delayed 25% frost projectile; the cage light contracts before release and a landed hit applies Chill (10% Speed reduction for two hero opportunities, max two stacks). No lamp meter or ability-resource drain. Wren marks the cage, Tobin guards the breath, Pip can use holy damage when unlocked; fire stays neutral.

**Art:** seven keys and a breath projectile. Stolen light changes brightness only; it is never a player-owned item consumed by combat.

### Wraithmarsh: Mire Seraph

**Pitch:** a hovering emissary with six reed-thin wings, a broken halo and a single spear grown through both forearms. Its wings resemble reeds because the darkness shaped it for this marsh, not because a bird was corrupted.

**Combat:** five hits; Speed 0.95; armour 10%; weak holy, resists poison. **Halo Thrust:** one 30% physical hit, delayed; the halo aligns with its spear immediately before contact. **Sixfold Lament:** three 9% frost pulses (27%), slow-slow-fast; paired wings close once per pulse. Six wings do not secretly mean six hits. Wren's setup can exploit its longer fight; Tobin's parries generate Grit; Pip's neutral fire is useful before holy abilities unlock.

**Art:** eight keys; three authored paired-wing states and one spear asset. Wings remain within a fixed arena envelope; no second actor, flight phase or unreachable melee target.

## Candidate priority, not a final selection

Highest excitement/buildability first: **Thorn Imp, Ossuary Knight, Lantern Eater, Riftwing, Shroudweaver, Riftforged Colossus, Mycelial Oracle, Mire Seraph, Cryptmaw, Maw Cantor, Seamstalker, Pall Reaper, Gloomjaw, Rot Herald.** This is a comparison order, not an instruction to drop an area's only candidate. Thorn Imp leads for the first-fight role; later enormous creatures should not eclipse the player's early scale.
