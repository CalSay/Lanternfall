## Shared calibration and card rules

- **Reference hero:** appropriately trained and geared for the zone. Toughness is the number of ordinary, uncritical Attack actions required to defeat the foe, with typical zone armour reflected. It is a target for step 3, not an attack-count guarantee for a trained finisher. Normals target 3–5 actions; named elite-base variants 6–8; elders 10–15; Ser Durand 15.
- **Speed:** ratios are against a reference hero at `1.0`. A foe at `1.10` is faster; `0.90` is slower. Ratios describe the scheduler, not a fixed extra move. The ordinary two-consecutive-opportunity limit applies; elders and the region boss may take at most three. A multi-hit move is still one foe opportunity.
- **Damage:** listed hits are a fixed percentage of reference hero maximum HP before Guard, Ward, and hero resistance. A notation such as `3 × 8% = 24%` gives per-hit damage and total direct damage. Normal single hits stay in the 20–35% band. Multi-hit normal moves split a similar total, so a full sequence does not require perfect defence to survive. Status ticks are additional only when written in the move. A parry or dodge is offered for every real hit.
- **Damage types:** physical armour is a flat reduction against physical hits only. Type weaknesses and resistances use the current references, `×1.5` and `×0.6`; all unlisted types are neutral. The five types are physical, holy, poison, fire, and frost. Every hit has exactly one type; a slash in an area's damage-lean description names the area's two common types, not a dual-type hit. “Light”, “medium”, and “heavy” armour below mean 10%, 20%, and 30% physical reduction respectively; these are calibration proposals.
- **Status:** incoming Burn is `4%` of reference hero HP at each of the next two hero starts; Weaken cuts direct output by 25% for the next hero opportunity; Blind affects the next player direct action but never hides a cue or forces a random reaction; Chill slows reference Speed by 10% per listed stack for two hero opportunities, at most two stacks. A move applies a status once, on the named hit, never once per hit by default. A landed hit is required. The player can answer through their selected defence, a relevant cleanse/ability, or by ending the fight.
- **Variants:** every named variant inherits all base-card fields unless its line gives a replacement. It is one foe, not an add. Its stated 6–8-action toughness is the final elite-base target with one elite trait included; do not apply a second HP multiplier for being both a named variant and an elite. A variant may roll with one trait from the shared elite table. Its pose silhouette can reuse its base.
- **Charges:** each elder and Ser Durand has one charged move. Starting it spends one foe opportunity and deals no damage. The release occurs on the next eligible foe opportunity after at least one hero opportunity. If Speed would release sooner, hold the move and let the hero respond without an extra damaging move. Interrupt before release with a non-locked Stun/Freeze contribution or `6%` of boss maximum HP in actual HP damage after the charge begins; Ward damage does not count, DoT HP damage does. An interrupted charge consumes the move and causes one recovery opportunity. It does not also grant a second skip. One ordinary move must resolve before another charge can begin. A phase transition cannot cancel the hero's response or grant an immediate attack.
- **Art budget:** a normal uses five to seven keys: idle, readable wind-ups, contact frames, hurt, and defeated. Reuse contact frames for hits with different cue timing. Status colours or small overlays do not require new poses. Each elder and the Knight gets a seven-key base set; phase changes reuse those poses and change light, marks, or framing. These are key counts, not generated or approved sprites.

## Region boss: Ser Durand, the Pyre Knight

**Story:** Ser Durand was Caedmon's shield-brother on the Emberlea road. The Voice offered them the same bargain. Caedmon refused; Durand said yes. He is not guarding a lamp and the fight does not reward that belief: he is the dark's grip on the Lea's stolen lights. Defeating him lets those lights go home. He returns to himself; he is not killed or consumed. The Ashen Wyrm is a separate world-raid foe and does not appear as a second combatant.

**Card:** speed `1.08`; toughness 15 hero actions; medium armour (20% physical reduction); weak frost `×1.5`, resists fire `×0.6`, neutral physical/holy/poison. His fire cannot harm Caedmon by a special exemption in solo combat; instead all heroes use the same authored defence. This keeps the one-on-one fight consistent. If Caedmon is the active hero, the rival line is a spoken cue only; the old five-second two-actor duel is deferred because no second actor may enter this stage.

After interruption, one ordinary move resolves before another charge can start. Phase changes occur at move end and cannot erase a pending hero response. Speed never permits more than three consecutive boss opportunities.

**Interrupt and counterplay:** all four moves allow a chosen parry or dodge for every real hit. The single- and multi-hit budgets do not require a perfect string. Parrying every hit of one move yields one full-move counter; parrying only some still refunds cooldowns per successful hit but yields no counter. Stun/Freeze uses boss Stagger under C19; interruption through the control path follows the shared control-lock rule. Do not add a second skip to a charge recovery.

**Hero matchups:** Wren can use Mark, Pinned, and an armour-piercing branch to make Cinder Guard's mitigation manageable; a multi-hit Volley gives more reaction opportunities but not extra enemy damage. Tobin can Brace before Oath Cut, gain cooldown refunds from each parried hit, and Sunder the armour; Riposte is lit by a successful parry, while the full counter still requires the entire enemy move to be parried. Pip should lead with Frost Shard or another non-fire option, then use Burn only when the target's resistance is accounted for. All three can interrupt the charge through actual HP damage or accepted control.

**Story beats:**

- **Before the first hit:** when Caedmon is the active hero, Durand says, “You walked away. I stayed.” Otherwise the versus card names Ser Durand and the Pyre; it does not invent a second actor or a new origin for the offer.
- **At the first phase change:** the shield's red seam opens onto the small lights held behind it. It makes clear he is the grip, not their keeper. No individual lamp speaks or is used as a damage resource.
- **On defeat:** the dark leaves Durand and he falls alive. If Caedmon is present in the story beat, preserve the lore line: “He agreed. I did not. I should have dragged him out.” The lamps rise toward their homes; the Great Lantern in Emberlea gives them a way back. The Lea gets real daylight, something green grows, and Caedmon sits at the table. The Pyre Knight's helm is later buried at the edge of the Lea.

## Gauntlet, elite-base and tuning notes

- Each listed area gauntlet is a 3–5 member sequence, fought one at a time. The player chooses whether to continue between members. Cooldowns, resources, and statuses reset per foe; HP follows the agreed healing model. No auto-start or offline reward is implied.
- A named variant is a tougher cousin of one area foe. Its change is shown on the variant line; unspecified cues, armour, types, matchup answers, and rewards inherit the base. When promoted, one elite trait attaches to that variant; it never summons a second actor. The elite HP target includes that trait.
- The seven shared elite traits, champion rules, Deepwell roster and existing Hunting beasts live in `enemies-c22-systems.md`, not this regional roster. Their effects must still obey the single-foe stage and the C22 charge/defence contract.

Check fire-resistance counterplay for Pip, armour counterplay for Wren/Tobin, per-hit defence and cooldown refunds, the 6% charge interrupt, phase transitions, status refreshes, and the ordinary/boss consecutive-opportunity caps. Adjust fixed zone numbers if the measured spread asks for it; do not increase percentages when a player's HP upgrade changes.
