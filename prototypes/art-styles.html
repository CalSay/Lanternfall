Pick an art direction

The same moment in the Barrow Downs, drawn three ways: the Warden, Old Hesketh and Pip against a Gloomhound and the Barrow Wight. Each stage runs live.

A

Hi-bit

Pixel art at high resolution: small crisp pixels, 4-tone hue-shifted shading, soft bloom and parallax.

What it means for the game

  • Look: keeps the pixel identity but with real definition: faces, armour highlights, cloth folds. Lighting is smooth, not pixelated.
  • Work: the most per character. About 48px sprites with 4 tones need roughly 3x the drawing time of today's 16px maps for each pose. 16 characters and 7 zones is a large but steady art job.
  • Performance: sprites are baked once and drawn as images, so a low-end phone runs it well. Bloom costs a few gradient draws per frame.

B

Illustrated

No visible pixels: smooth vector shapes, clean dark outlines, cel shading and a painted backdrop.

What it means for the game

  • Look: the biggest change. Crisp at any screen size, reads like a modern mobile RPG. Loses the pixel charm the game has today.
  • Work: characters are built from shapes and a shared rig, so new characters and poses are quick once the rig exists; smooth motion (squash, sway) comes almost free.
  • Performance: every character is redrawn as paths each frame. Fine on mid phones; on low-end ones we would cache poses to images.

C

Refined pixel

Today's style cleaned up: 32px characters at 2x, outline only on the outside edge, clearer faces, smooth lighting on top.

What it means for the game

  • Look: closest to what players know. Clearer than today, but still chunky at phone size.
  • Work: the smallest step. Sprites double in size and gain a third tone; the current renderer, palettes and stage code mostly carry over.
  • Performance: the cheapest of the three. Runs on anything.
